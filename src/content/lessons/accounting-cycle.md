## Keep the equation balanced

Assets = liabilities + equity. Assets are controlled resources, liabilities are obligations, and equity is the owners’ remaining claim. Revenue raises equity; expenses and withdrawals reduce it. Owner investment is not sales revenue, and borrowing is not profit. Analyze the transaction before choosing an account.

A chart of accounts gives each account a consistent name, category, and often a number. Classify cash and receivables as assets, payables as liabilities, owner capital as equity, earned fees as revenue, and rent as expense. This classification determines the normal balance before you write the entry.

> **Example**
>
> An owner invests $600, then buys $90 of supplies for cash.
>
> Cash becomes $510 and supplies $90. Assets and equity remain $600. Supplies are not an expense until consumed.

## Journalize and post

Debits are left entries and credits are right entries. Assets and expenses normally increase with debits; liabilities, equity, and revenue normally increase with credits. Record evidence in the journal, then post to ledger accounts. Special journals group recurring transactions. A trial balance compares totals but cannot detect every omitted transaction or wrong account.

## Adjust, report, close

Accrual accounting recognizes revenue when earned and expenses in the appropriate period. Adjust expired prepayments, used supplies, earned advances, depreciation, and unpaid earned wages before reporting. A worksheet organizes adjustments but is not a statement. Close temporary revenue, expense, and withdrawal/dividend accounts; permanent assets and liabilities carry forward. Never close cash merely because a year ends.

For a $120 twelve-month prepaid policy, one month of coverage consumes $10: debit insurance expense and credit prepaid insurance. For $75 wages earned but unpaid, debit wages expense and credit wages payable. Adjustments are not the same as closing: adjustments make period amounts accurate; closing resets temporary accounts for the next period.

## Key terms

**Ledger.** The collection of accounts and their balances.

**Accrual.** Recognizing activity when earned or incurred rather than only when cash moves.
