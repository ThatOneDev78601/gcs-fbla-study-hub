## The ten steps in order

Objective tests often ask which step comes next or which step a document belongs to. Learn the order as a chain where each step uses the output of the one before it. Source documents prove that a transaction happened. The journal records each transaction in date order. The ledger groups those entries by account. The trial balance tests whether total debits equal total credits.

Adjusting entries come after the unadjusted trial balance and before the statements. They are made at the end of the period and never involve cash. Closing entries come after the statements because the statements need the revenue and expense balances before they are reset to zero.

1. Collect and analyze source documents such as invoices, receipts, checks, and deposit slips.
2. Journalize each transaction in the general journal or a special journal.
3. Post journal amounts to ledger accounts.
4. Prepare the unadjusted trial balance.
5. Record and post adjusting entries.
6. Prepare the adjusted trial balance, often on a worksheet.
7. Prepare the income statement, statement of owner's equity, and balance sheet.
8. Journalize and post closing entries.
9. Prepare the post-closing trial balance.
10. Optionally record reversing entries on the first day of the next period.

## Record a month of transactions

Lantern Lawn Care is a fictional sole proprietorship that starts business on June 1. The table shows each transaction, the entry, and the reason. Read the reason column carefully. The most common test errors come from treating owner investment as revenue, treating a customer advance as revenue, or treating a prepayment as an expense.

| Transaction | Debit | Credit | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Owner invests $5,000 cash | Cash 5,000 | Owner's Capital 5,000 | Owner investment raises equity but is not revenue. |
| 2. Buys equipment for $2,400: $600 cash, rest on account | Equipment 2,400 | Cash 600; Accounts Payable 1,800 | One entry can have several credits if the totals match. |
| 3. Pays $1,200 for a 12-month insurance policy | Prepaid Insurance 1,200 | Cash 1,200 | The coverage has not been used yet, so it is an asset. |
| 4. Buys supplies on account, $300 | Supplies 300 | Accounts Payable 300 | Supplies are an asset until used. |
| 5. Performs services for cash, $1,500 | Cash 1,500 | Service Revenue 1,500 | Revenue is earned when the work is done. |
| 6. Performs services on account, $900 | Accounts Receivable 900 | Service Revenue 900 | Earned now, collected later. |
| 7. Receives $400 in advance for July work | Cash 400 | Unearned Revenue 400 | The business owes the work, so this is a liability. |
| 8. Pays wages, $700 | Wages Expense 700 | Cash 700 | Wages for work already performed. |
| 9. Collects $500 from customers on account | Cash 500 | Accounts Receivable 500 | One asset rises and another falls. No revenue. |
| 10. Pays $1,000 on account | Accounts Payable 1,000 | Cash 1,000 | Paying a debt is not an expense. |
| 11. Owner withdraws $300 | Owner's Drawing 300 | Cash 300 | Withdrawals reduce equity but are not expenses. |

> **Example**
>
> After posting, what is the Cash balance, and does the unadjusted trial balance balance?
>
> 1. Cash: 5,000 - 600 - 1,200 + 1,500 + 400 - 700 + 500 - 1,000 - 300 = 3,600.
> 2. Accounts Receivable: 900 - 500 = 400. Accounts Payable: 1,800 + 300 - 1,000 = 1,100.
> 3. Debit balances: Cash 3,600 + A/R 400 + Supplies 300 + Prepaid Insurance 1,200 + Equipment 2,400 + Drawing 300 + Wages Expense 700 = 8,900.
> 4. Credit balances: A/P 1,100 + Unearned Revenue 400 + Capital 5,000 + Service Revenue 2,400 = 8,900.
>
> Cash is $3,600 and both columns total $8,900, so the unadjusted trial balance is in balance. A balanced trial balance still does not prove every entry is correct.

## Adjust at the end of the month

Each adjustment fixes the timing of a revenue or expense so the month shows what was actually earned and used. Every adjusting entry affects one income statement account and one balance sheet account. Cash is never part of an adjusting entry.

Lantern counts $120 of supplies left on June 30, so $180 was used. One month of the 12-month policy has expired, which is $1,200 / 12 = $100. Equipment costing $2,400 with a 4-year life and no salvage value depreciates $600 a year, or $50 a month. Lantern completed $150 of the work customers paid for in advance. Employees earned $200 of wages that will be paid in July. Lantern also finished $250 of work that has not been billed yet.

| Adjustment | Debit | Credit | Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a. Supplies used | Supplies Expense 180 | Supplies 180 | Deferred expense |
| b. Insurance expired | Insurance Expense 100 | Prepaid Insurance 100 | Deferred expense |
| c. Depreciation | Depreciation Expense 50 | Accumulated Depreciation 50 | Estimate |
| d. Advance earned | Unearned Revenue 150 | Service Revenue 150 | Deferred revenue |
| e. Wages earned, unpaid | Wages Expense 200 | Wages Payable 200 | Accrued expense |
| f. Work done, unbilled | Accounts Receivable 250 | Service Revenue 250 | Accrued revenue |

- Accumulated Depreciation is a contra-asset with a credit balance. Book value is cost minus accumulated depreciation: 2,400 - 50 = 2,350.
- After adjustment, Service Revenue is 2,400 + 150 + 250 = 2,800 and Wages Expense is 700 + 200 = 900.
- The adjusted trial balance totals $9,400 on each side.

## Prepare the three statements

Prepare the statements in this order because each one feeds the next. Net income from the income statement goes to the statement of owner's equity. Ending capital from that statement goes to the balance sheet.

Income statement: Service Revenue 2,800. Expenses are Wages 900, Supplies 180, Insurance 100, and Depreciation 50, for a total of 1,230. Net income is 1,570.

Statement of owner's equity: Beginning capital 0, plus investment 5,000, plus net income 1,570, minus drawing 300, equals ending capital 6,270.

Balance sheet: Cash 3,600, Accounts Receivable 650, Supplies 120, Prepaid Insurance 1,100, and Equipment at book value 2,350 give total assets of 7,820. Liabilities are Accounts Payable 1,100, Wages Payable 200, and Unearned Revenue 250, for 1,550. Liabilities 1,550 plus equity 6,270 equal 7,820, so the equation balances.

## Close the books

Closing moves the period's revenue, expense, and drawing balances into capital so those temporary accounts start July at zero. Permanent accounts, which are the balance sheet accounts, carry forward. Many textbooks use an Income Summary account as a temporary holding place.

1. Close revenue: debit Service Revenue 2,800; credit Income Summary 2,800.
2. Close expenses: debit Income Summary 1,230; credit each expense for its balance.
3. Close Income Summary: debit Income Summary 1,570; credit Owner's Capital 1,570. A net loss would reverse this entry.
4. Close drawing: debit Owner's Capital 300; credit Owner's Drawing 300. Drawing never goes through Income Summary.

> **Example**
>
> Which accounts appear on Lantern's post-closing trial balance, and what are the totals?
>
> Only permanent accounts appear. Debits: Cash 3,600, A/R 650, Supplies 120, Prepaid Insurance 1,100, Equipment 2,400, totaling 7,870. Credits: Accumulated Depreciation 50, A/P 1,100, Wages Payable 200, Unearned Revenue 250, Capital 6,270, totaling 7,870. Total assets on the balance sheet are lower, at 7,820, because the balance sheet subtracts accumulated depreciation while the trial balance lists it as a credit.

## Find and correct errors

A trial balance catches errors that make debits and credits unequal. It does not catch an omitted transaction, an entry recorded twice, an entry posted to the wrong account on the correct side, or two errors that cancel. Use the size of the difference as a clue.

To correct an error after posting, write a correcting entry. Do not erase. If a $450 utility bill was recorded as Rent Expense, debit Utilities Expense 450 and credit Rent Expense 450. Cash was correct, so it is not touched.

- Difference divisible by 9: look for a transposition such as 96 recorded as 69 (difference 27) or a slide such as 450 recorded as 45.
- Difference equal to twice an amount: an amount was posted to the wrong side. A $60 debit posted as a credit creates a $120 difference.
- Difference equal to one amount: one side of an entry was not posted.

> **Try it yourself**
>
> Birch Tutoring's unadjusted balances include Service Revenue 3,000, Rent Expense 800, Supplies 400, and Prepaid Insurance 600 for a 6-month policy bought this month. Beginning capital is 2,000 and drawing is 500. At month end, $150 of supplies remain and employees earned $120 of unpaid wages. Record the adjustments, then find net income and ending capital.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

Supplies Expense 250 / Supplies 250 (400 - 150). Insurance Expense 100 / Prepaid Insurance 100 (600 / 6). Wages Expense 120 / Wages Payable 120. Total expenses are 800 + 250 + 100 + 120 = 1,270. Net income is 3,000 - 1,270 = 1,730. Ending capital is 2,000 + 1,730 - 500 = 3,230.

</details>

## Key terms

**Adjusted trial balance.** A list of all account balances after adjusting entries, used to prepare the financial statements.

**Contra-asset.** An account with a credit balance that reduces a related asset, such as Accumulated Depreciation.

**Income Summary.** A temporary account used during closing to collect revenues and expenses before the net amount moves to capital.

**Post-closing trial balance.** A trial balance prepared after closing that lists only permanent accounts.

**Correcting entry.** A new journal entry that fixes an error already posted, leaving the original record visible.
