## Who sets the rules

Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) are the rules U.S. businesses follow for financial reports. The Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) sets GAAP for businesses and nonprofits. The Governmental Accounting Standards Board (GASB) sets standards for state and local governments. The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has legal authority over public company reporting and relies on FASB. The International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) issues IFRS, which most other countries use.

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 followed the Enron and WorldCom frauds. It created the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) to oversee auditors of public companies, requires executives to certify financial reports, and requires management to report on internal control over financial reporting.

| Concept | Meaning | Quick example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economic entity | Business records are kept separate from the owner's | The owner's personal car payment is not a business expense |
| Going concern | Assume the business will keep operating | Equipment is depreciated over its useful life instead of shown at liquidation value |
| Monetary unit | Record only items measurable in money | Employee morale is not recorded as an asset |
| Periodicity | Divide the business's life into reporting periods | Monthly, quarterly, and annual statements |
| Historical cost | Record assets at their purchase cost | Land bought for $80,000 stays at $80,000 even if its value rises |
| Revenue recognition | Record revenue when the performance obligation is satisfied | A gym records a yearly fee one month at a time |
| Matching (expense recognition) | Record expenses in the period of the revenue they helped earn | Sales commissions are recorded in the month of the sale |
| Full disclosure | Report information that would change a user's decision | A pending lawsuit is described in the notes |

- The two fundamental qualities of useful information are relevance and faithful representation.
- The enhancing qualities are comparability, verifiability, timeliness, and understandability.

## Tax accounting records

Sales tax collected from customers belongs to the state, so it is a liability. Maryland's general sales tax rate has been 6%; use the rate the question gives. A $500 taxable sale is recorded as debit Cash 530, credit Sales 500, credit Sales Tax Payable 30. Paying the state debits Sales Tax Payable and credits Cash. Sales tax is never revenue.

The form of business decides how income tax is filed. A sole proprietor reports business profit on Schedule C with the owner's Form 1040 and pays self-employment tax. A partnership files Form 1065 and gives each partner a Schedule K-1; the partners pay the tax. A C corporation files Form 1120 and pays a flat 21% federal rate under current law. An S corporation files Form 1120-S and passes income to shareholders. Owners without withholding generally make quarterly estimated payments.

Tax rules and financial accounting rules can differ. A company may use straight-line depreciation for its statements and the faster MACRS method on its tax return. Differences that reverse over time create deferred tax amounts. The IRS generally recommends keeping records supporting a return for three years after filing, longer in some situations, and employment tax records for at least four years.

> **Example**
>
> A client who runs a small bakery wants to deduct the family's grocery bill and has lost several supplier receipts.
>
> Explain that personal expenses are not business deductions under the economic entity concept and tax law. Help the client rebuild records from bank and card statements and supplier copies, and set up a system that separates business and personal accounts. Do not invent amounts or dates.

## Accounting software and its controls

Accounting software records the same debits and credits as a paper journal, but it posts automatically and produces reports instantly. Setting it up correctly matters more than learning menus. Build the chart of accounts first, enter opening balances, connect bank feeds if available, and set user permissions so that the person who records bills is not the person who approves payments.

An audit trail, sometimes called audit history, records who created, changed, or deleted each transaction and when. A good practice is to correct a posted transaction with a reversing or adjusting entry instead of editing it, especially after a period is closed. Most programs let an administrator set a closing date and require a password to change anything before it.

1. Enter a sample invoice, a bill, and a payment in a practice company file.
2. Run the trial balance, income statement, and accounts receivable aging report, and check that totals match your manual calculation.
3. Correct one deliberate error with an adjusting entry, then open the audit trail and find both the original and the correction.
4. Back up the company file, change a record, restore the backup, and confirm the change is gone. A backup that has never been restored has not been tested.
5. Set a closing date and confirm that a new entry dated in the closed period is blocked or requires approval.

## Annual reports and disclosure

A public company's annual report to the SEC is Form 10-K. It includes a business description, risk factors, management's discussion and analysis (MD&A), audited financial statements, notes, and the auditor's report. The notes explain accounting policies, such as the inventory method, and give details that the statements cannot show, such as debt terms and lawsuits.

The auditor's opinion tells readers how much to trust the statements. An unmodified (clean) opinion says the statements are fairly presented. A qualified opinion says they are fair except for a specific matter. An adverse opinion says they are not fairly presented. A disclaimer means the auditor could not form an opinion.

## Careers and professional organizations

Accounting careers include public accounting (audit, tax, and advisory work for clients), corporate or managerial accounting, government accounting, and forensic accounting. The Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook gives current duties, pay, and job outlook.

## Key terms

**GAAP.** Generally accepted accounting principles, the U.S. rules for financial reporting.

**FASB.** Financial Accounting Standards Board, the private body that sets U.S. GAAP for businesses and nonprofits.

**Audit trail.** A record showing who entered or changed each transaction and when.

**Form 10-K.** The annual report a public company files with the SEC.

**Unmodified opinion.** An auditor's conclusion that the financial statements are fairly presented.
