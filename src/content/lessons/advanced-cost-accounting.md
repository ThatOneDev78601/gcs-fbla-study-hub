## Assign inventory costs

Cost of goods sold = beginning inventory + net purchases − ending inventory. Perpetual systems update through the period; periodic systems rely on a period-end calculation. FIFO assigns earlier purchase costs to sales first; weighted average spreads available cost across units. These are cost assumptions, not necessarily physical shipping instructions.

LIFO assigns latest costs to sales first. In the 20-unit example, LIFO cost of 12 sold is $68: 10 × $6 + 2 × $4; ending inventory is $32. Shrinkage means recorded inventory exceeds the physical count; adjust the record rather than continuing to report missing goods. Returns and obsolescence also require appropriate adjustments.

> **Example**
>
> Buy 10 units at $4 and 10 at $6, then sell 12.
>
> FIFO cost of goods sold is $52: 10 × $4 + 2 × $6. Ending inventory is $48. Weighted-average unit cost is $5.

## Separate gross and net pay

Gross pay is compensation before deductions; net pay is after employee deductions. Withheld taxes remain liabilities until remitted. Employer taxes and benefits add labor cost beyond the employee’s check. Accrue earned, unpaid wages at period end. Use supplied tax rates and wage bases for exam calculations rather than memorizing outdated annual thresholds.

For a practice worker earning $15 per hour, 40 regular hours and two overtime hours at a stated 1.5 multiplier produce $645 gross pay: $600 + $45. If specified deductions total $95, net pay is $550. Employee deductions and employer payroll taxes need separate records and remittance. Social Security, Medicare, unemployment taxes, benefits, and workers’ compensation can affect employer costs; use the problem’s assumptions.

## Use relevant costs

Fixed costs stay constant within a relevant range; variable costs change with activity. Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit. Break-even units = fixed costs / contribution per unit, rounding up to a feasible unit. A sunk cost is already incurred; it should not distort a future choice. Include opportunity costs and avoid treating an allocated average as incremental cost.

Variance analysis compares actual results with a budget or standard. A $500 cost budget and $560 actual cost produce a $60 unfavorable cost variance. First check whether actual activity differs from planned activity; a flexible budget makes the comparison at a comparable activity level.

> **Example**
>
> Price is $20, variable cost $12, and fixed costs $240.
>
> Each unit contributes $8. Break-even is 30 units. At 40 units, $320 contribution less $240 fixed costs leaves $80.

## Key terms

**Sunk cost.** A past cost unaffected by the current choice.

**Contribution margin.** Revenue remaining after variable costs.
