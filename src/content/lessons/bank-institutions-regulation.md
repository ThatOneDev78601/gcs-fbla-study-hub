## Money and the financial system

Money has three functions. It is a medium of exchange, a unit of account for comparing values, and a store of value. Good money is durable, portable, divisible, uniform, limited in supply, and widely accepted. Banks are financial intermediaries: they take deposits from savers and lend to borrowers, earning the spread between the interest they charge and the interest they pay.

The money market trades short-term debt of one year or less, such as Treasury bills and commercial paper. The capital market trades long-term stocks and bonds. In the primary market, securities are sold for the first time and the money goes to the issuer. In the secondary market, investors trade with each other.

| Institution | Owned by | Main activity | Deposit insurance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial bank | Shareholders | Deposits, business and consumer loans | FDIC |
| Savings institution (thrift) | Shareholders or depositors | Historically home mortgages | FDIC |
| Credit union | Members, as a not-for-profit cooperative | Member deposits (shares) and loans | NCUA, through the National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund |
| Investment bank | Shareholders | Underwriting securities, mergers advice | None for investments |
| Insurance company | Shareholders or policyholders | Risk protection; invests premiums | State guaranty associations |
| Finance company | Shareholders | Consumer and business loans; no deposits | None |

- FDIC and NCUA insurance covers up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured institution, for each ownership category.
- Consolidation means fewer, larger institutions through mergers. Reasons include economies of scale, technology costs, and geographic reach. Concerns include less local lending and institutions that are too big to fail.

## A timeline of U.S. banking

Tests often ask which law created an agency or ended a practice. Learn each law by the problem it answered.

| Year | Event | What it did |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1791 | First Bank of the United States | A national bank promoted by Alexander Hamilton; its charter ended in 1811 |
| 1816 | Second Bank of the United States | Rechartered a national bank; President Andrew Jackson opposed it and its charter ended in 1836 |
| 1863-1864 | National Currency and National Bank Acts | Created national bank charters, a national currency, and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) |
| 1913 | Federal Reserve Act | Created the Federal Reserve as the central bank after repeated banking panics |
| 1933 | Banking Act (Glass-Steagall) | Created the FDIC and separated commercial banking from investment banking |
| 1934 | Federal Credit Union Act | Allowed federally chartered credit unions |
| 1977 | Community Reinvestment Act | Requires banks to help meet credit needs of their whole community, including low- and moderate-income areas |
| 1994 | Riegle-Neal Act | Allowed interstate banking and branching |
| 1999 | Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act | Repealed the Glass-Steagall separation and set financial privacy rules |
| 2010 | Dodd-Frank Act | Created the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) and the Financial Stability Oversight Council after the 2008 crisis |

## The regulators

The United States has a dual banking system. A bank can hold a national charter from the OCC or a state charter from a state banking department. In Maryland, the state regulator is the Office of Financial Regulation. Every bank also has a federal regulator.

The Federal Reserve has a Board of Governors in Washington with seven members serving 14-year terms, and 12 regional Reserve Banks. The Federal Open Market Committee sets monetary policy. The Fed's tools are open market operations (buying and selling government securities), the discount rate charged on loans to banks, and interest paid on reserve balances. Reserve requirements have been set at zero since March 2020.

| Agency | Supervises or does |
| --- | --- |
| Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) | Charters and supervises national banks |
| Federal Reserve | Central bank; supervises state member banks and bank holding companies |
| FDIC | Insures deposits; supervises state banks that are not Fed members; resolves failed banks |
| NCUA | Charters federal credit unions and insures credit union shares |
| CFPB | Writes and enforces consumer financial protection rules for large institutions and nonbank lenders |
| FinCEN | Treasury bureau that enforces anti-money-laundering rules |

## Lending, operations, and electronic rules

Compliance questions usually describe a situation and ask which rule applies. Group the rules by the activity they control.

| Rule | Controls | Key point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truth in Lending Act (Regulation Z) | Credit disclosures | Lenders must disclose the APR and finance charge before credit is extended |
| Equal Credit Opportunity Act (Regulation B) | Fair lending | No discrimination based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, or receipt of public assistance |
| Fair Credit Reporting Act | Credit reports | Accuracy, consumer access, and notice when a report leads to denial |
| Home Mortgage Disclosure Act | Mortgage data | Lenders report data used to detect discriminatory patterns |
| Truth in Savings Act (Regulation DD) | Deposit accounts | Disclose fees and the annual percentage yield (APY) |
| Expedited Funds Availability Act (Regulation CC) | Check holds | Sets the maximum time a bank may hold deposited funds; dollar amounts are adjusted for inflation |
| Electronic Fund Transfer Act (Regulation E) | Debit cards, ATM, transfers | Error resolution and limits on consumer liability, which depend on how quickly a lost card is reported |
| Bank Secrecy Act and USA PATRIOT Act | Money laundering | Currency Transaction Report for cash over $10,000 in a day; Suspicious Activity Reports; customer identification |
| E-SIGN Act | Electronic records | Electronic signatures and disclosures are valid when the consumer consents |

- Structuring, which means splitting cash deposits to avoid a Currency Transaction Report, is itself a federal crime. Tellers may not advise customers how to avoid a report.
- The Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act requires privacy notices and safeguards for customer information.

> **Example**
>
> A loan officer asks an applicant whether she plans to have children before approving a car loan.
>
> This violates the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, which bars decisions based on sex and marital status and limits questions about childbearing plans. The officer should evaluate income, debts, credit history, and collateral only.

## Key terms

**Financial intermediary.** An institution that moves funds from savers to borrowers.

**Dual banking system.** The U.S. arrangement in which banks may be chartered by either the federal government or a state.

**Open market operations.** The Federal Reserve's buying and selling of government securities to influence interest rates.

**Currency Transaction Report.** A report banks file for cash transactions over $10,000 in one business day.

**APY.** Annual percentage yield: the yearly return on a deposit including compounding.
