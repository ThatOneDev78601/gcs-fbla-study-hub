## Check and cash handling

A check's bottom line is the MICR line, printed in magnetic ink. It shows the 9-digit routing number that identifies the bank, the account number, and the check number. Before cashing a check, a teller verifies the customer's identity, confirms the written amount matches the numeric amount, checks the date and signature, and confirms that funds are available when the check is drawn on the teller's own bank.

Endorsements transfer a check. A blank endorsement is only a signature, so anyone holding the check can cash it. A restrictive endorsement such as "For deposit only" limits further use and is the safest choice for mailed deposits. A special endorsement names a new payee: "Pay to the order of Jordan Lee," then the signature. A check more than six months old is stale-dated, and a bank may refuse it. A postdated check carries a future date.

- Counterfeit currency warning signs: missing security thread or watermark, color-shifting ink that does not shift, and flat, blurry printing.
- Check fraud warning signs: altered amounts or payee names, a new account receiving large deposits followed by quick withdrawals, and overpayment requests where a stranger asks the customer to send back the difference.
- Elder financial exploitation warning signs: an older customer making unusual withdrawals while accompanied by someone who answers for them. Follow the bank's escalation procedure.

## Balance a teller drawer

At the start of a shift, the teller counts and signs for a starting amount. During the day, the teller records cash received and paid out. At the end, the expected balance is the beginning cash plus cash in minus cash out. The teller counts the drawer and compares. A difference is recorded as cash over or cash short and reported to a supervisor. A teller never covers a shortage with personal money.

> **Example**
>
> A drawer starts with $2,500. During the shift the teller receives $3,180 in cash deposits and loan payments and pays out $2,940 in withdrawals and cashed checks. The ending count is $2,730.
>
> 1. Expected: 2,500 + 3,180 - 2,940 = 2,740.
> 2. Difference: 2,730 - 2,740 = -10.
>
> The drawer is $10 short. The teller recounts, reviews the day's transactions for a keying error, and reports the shortage under the bank's policy.

## Analyze a loan application

Lenders judge credit using the five Cs. Character is the borrower's history of repaying, shown in the credit report and score. Capacity is the ability to repay from income, measured by the debt-to-income ratio. Capital is the borrower's own money in the deal, such as a down payment. Collateral is property the lender can claim if the loan is not repaid. Conditions are the loan's purpose and the economic environment.

Debt-to-income (DTI) equals monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. Loan-to-value (LTV) equals the loan amount divided by the appraised value of the collateral. Lower DTI and LTV mean lower risk.

Loan structure includes the term, the payment schedule, whether the loan is secured, and whether the rate is fixed or variable. A variable rate is often set as an index such as the prime rate plus a margin. Risk-based pricing charges a higher rate to borrowers with weaker credit. Explain the terms to the client in plain language and give the required disclosures.

> **Example**
>
> An applicant earns $6,000 a month before taxes. Monthly debts would be a $1,500 mortgage, a $400 car payment, and a $300 student loan. A home appraises at $250,000 and the requested mortgage is $225,000.
>
> DTI: (1,500 + 400 + 300) / 6,000 = 36.7%. LTV: 225,000 / 250,000 = 90%. Many lenders treat 36% to 43% DTI as a review range and may require mortgage insurance when LTV is above 80%. The specific limits depend on the loan program.

## Banking calculations

Simple interest is I = P x r x t. A $5,000 loan at 8% for 9 months costs 5,000 x 0.08 x 0.75 = $300. Compound interest earns interest on interest: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt). APY converts a stated rate to its yearly effect: APY = (1 + r/n)^n - 1. A 4.8% rate compounded monthly has an APY of (1.004)^12 - 1 = 4.91%.

A fixed loan payment is P x i / (1 - (1 + i)^-n), where i is the periodic rate and n is the number of payments. The rule of 72 estimates how long money takes to double: 72 divided by the interest rate. At 6%, money doubles in about 12 years.

> **Example**
>
> Compare two $20,000 auto loans: 6% for 60 months, or 5.5% for 72 months.
>
> 1. 60-month loan: i = 0.06 / 12 = 0.005 and n = 60. (1.005)^-60 = 0.74137. Payment = 20,000 x 0.005 / (1 - 0.74137) = 100 / 0.25863 = $386.66.
> 2. 60-month total paid: 60 x 386.66 = $23,199.60, so interest is $3,199.60.
> 3. 72-month loan: i = 0.055 / 12 = 0.0045833 and n = 72. Payment = $326.76. Total paid: 72 x 326.76 = $23,526.72, so interest is $3,526.72.
>
> The 72-month loan has a lower rate and a payment about $60 lower, yet it costs $327 more in interest because the balance is outstanding for a year longer. Compare the APR and the total cost, as well as the monthly payment.

## Commercial products and careers

Business customers use term loans for equipment, lines of credit for seasonal cash needs, commercial real estate loans, and SBA-guaranteed loans. Treasury management services include ACH payments, positive pay (the bank matches checks presented against a list the business issued), remote deposit capture, lockbox collection, and merchant card services. Letters of credit guarantee payment in international trade.

Banking careers include teller, personal banker, loan officer, credit analyst, underwriter, compliance officer, BSA/AML analyst, and branch manager. Professional groups include the American Bankers Association (ABA), the Independent Community Bankers of America (ICBA), and state groups such as the Maryland Bankers Association. Credentials include the Certified Regulatory Compliance Manager (CRCM) from the ABA and the Certified Anti-Money Laundering Specialist (CAMS) from ACAMS.

## Key terms

**Restrictive endorsement.** An endorsement such as "For deposit only" that limits how a check can be used.

**Debt-to-income ratio.** Monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income.

**Loan-to-value ratio.** The loan amount divided by the appraised value of the collateral.

**Five Cs of credit.** Character, capacity, capital, collateral, and conditions.

**Positive pay.** A fraud control in which the bank pays only checks that match the business's issued-check list.
