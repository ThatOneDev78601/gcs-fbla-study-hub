## Explain what institutions do

Financial institutions connect savers, borrowers, payments, and investment activity. Commercial banks and credit unions offer deposit and lending services under different ownership structures. Central banks serve a different role in money and credit conditions. Checking accounts support transactions; savings accounts support reserves; term deposits trade flexibility for stated terms. Deposits and investments have different risk and protection structures. Never assume every product sold in a bank carries deposit insurance.

Money markets generally concern short-term debt instruments; capital markets concern longer-term financing such as stocks and bonds. Insurance markets pool specified risks, and commodity markets trade raw materials or related contracts. Industry consolidation combines institutions, while convergence brings previously separate services together. Retail banking serves individuals, business banking serves organizations, and universal banking combines a wider range of activities.

## Evaluate a loan and the balance sheet

Banks earn revenue through interest and fees while managing funding costs, credit losses, liquidity, and operating expenses. Credit risk concerns repayment; liquidity risk concerns meeting cash obligations when due. Underwriting evaluates capacity, credit history, collateral, and other relevant evidence. A secured loan has pledged collateral; it does not eliminate all borrower responsibility. Analyze ratios and cash flow together, because an asset-rich borrower can still struggle with near-term payments.

Loan pricing reflects funding cost, expected credit loss, operating cost, term, collateral, and competition. Business services can include payroll, merchant payment processing, cash management, and working-capital credit. Apply time-value formulas with matching periods: at hypothetical 5% annual compounding, $1,000 becomes $1,102.50 in two years.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A borrower has $2,000 monthly take-home income and $700 existing monthly debt payments.
>
> Existing debt payments consume 35% of take-home income. That observation alone is not approval: required underwriting definitions, additional costs, and lender criteria also matter.

## Daily operations and professional conduct

Reconcile transactions, verify authorization, safeguard customer information, and investigate discrepancies. Account-opening and monitoring procedures support identification and financial-crime controls. Follow the institution’s escalation process for suspicious activity rather than confronting someone or promising an outcome. Good customer relations combine listening, plain explanations of terms and fees, accurate records, and respectful complaint resolution. Careers include tellers, relationship specialists, loan officers, analysts, and compliance staff; each requires both technical competence and trust.

The Federal Reserve influences monetary conditions and supports banking-system functions; other banking, deposit-insurance, and consumer regulators have distinct responsibilities. Identify the correct authority for the issue rather than assuming one agency regulates every product. Teller standards include accurate balancing, authorization, privacy, and clear customer explanations. Professional associations and credentials support continuing learning; they do not replace institutional procedures.

## Key terms to remember

**Credit risk.** Risk that a borrower does not repay as agreed.

**Liquidity risk.** Risk of being unable to meet obligations when due.
