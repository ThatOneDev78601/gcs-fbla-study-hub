## Research careers and prepare applications

Career planning connects interests, strengths, values, and constraints with researched roles. Look at daily tasks and required skills, not only a job title. Set a development goal, practice it, and retain evidence. A résumé should communicate relevant accomplishments honestly; a cover letter explains fit for a particular role. Prepare interview examples using situation, task, action, and result. Do not invent experience or disclose another organization’s confidential information.

Research education, credentials, typical working conditions, and opportunities for growth. Networking means building reciprocal professional relationships, not requesting favors from strangers. An informational interview explores a role and should respect the other person’s time. Follow a job application’s instructions and check names, dates, contact details, and attachments before sending.

## Keep organized office records

Professional procedures include scheduling, meeting preparation, phone etiquette, filing, document naming, and version control. Use descriptive subjects and filenames so another person can locate the record. Confirm time zones, participants, deadlines, and responsibilities. Take concise notes of decisions and action owners. Handle visitors and calls respectfully while verifying identity before sharing information. Information management is about accuracy, retrievability, appropriate access, and retention as well as storage.

> **Example**
>
> A team has three files called final.docx and different meeting dates.
>
> Choose one controlled location, use clear version names, record the approved meeting time and time zone, and identify who can authorize changes.

## Workplace decisions and communication

Prioritize by impact, urgency, and dependencies. Follow workplace policy, use the correct approval route, and escalate issues beyond your authority. Protect confidential data with appropriate permissions; do not forward a whole customer file when only one fact is needed. Use strong authentication and report suspicious requests through established channels. Professional development continues after hiring: seek feedback, update skills, and document what improved. Reliable follow-through matters more than appearing constantly busy.

## Key terms

**Version control.** A method for tracking and identifying document changes.

**STAR.** Situation, task, action, result: a structure for explaining experience.
