## Begin with audience and purpose

Communication involves a sender, message, channel, receiver, feedback, and possible noise. Decide what the reader needs to know or do before drafting. Choose a channel appropriate to urgency, complexity, sensitivity, and the need for a record. A routine request can begin with its main point; sensitive or unfavorable news may need respectful context. Avoid unnecessary jargon and define essential terms.

## Construct clear sentences and documents

Use a clear subject, an accurate verb, and parallel structure. Match subjects and verbs, keep pronoun references clear, and distinguish commonly confused words. Revise long sentences into logical units. Business correspondence should identify the purpose, essential facts, requested action, and deadline. Reports separate findings from recommendations. Tables need informative headings and units. Cite sources and distinguish quotation, paraphrase, and your own conclusion.

A complete independent clause can stand alone; joining two with only a comma creates a comma splice. Use a period, a semicolon, or an appropriate conjunction. Keep items parallel: “researching, drafting, and revising,” rather than mixing grammatical forms. Use punctuation to clarify relationships, not decorate. Direct quotations require quotation marks and attribution; paraphrases still need a source when they rely on another person’s work.

> **Work through an example**
>
> Draft: “It has been determined that completion of the form should occur soon.”
>
> Revision: “Please submit the permission form by 3 p.m. Friday.” The revision supplies an actor, action, and deadline.

## Read, revise, and proofread

Read for the main claim, supporting evidence, assumptions, and implied meaning. A chart can be misleading through an omitted baseline, unequal intervals, or missing denominator. Revise first for organization and completeness, then edit sentences, then proofread spelling, names, numbers, and attachments. Check tone from the reader’s perspective. A spellchecker will not reliably catch a correctly spelled but wrong word. Preserve confidentiality and accessibility when formatting the final document.

## Key terms to remember

**Noise.** Anything that interferes with understanding a message.

**Parallel structure.** Using consistent grammatical forms for comparable ideas.
