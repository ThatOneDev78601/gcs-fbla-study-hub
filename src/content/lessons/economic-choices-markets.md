## Think at the margin

Scarcity means wants exceed available resources. Opportunity cost is the best alternative sacrificed, not all alternatives added together. Land, labor, productive capital, and entrepreneurship are resources. Economic capital means tools and equipment. Marginal analysis compares additional benefit with additional cost; a past payment should not dominate a future decision.

Goods are tangible items; services are activities. Business can add form utility by transforming inputs, place utility by moving a product where needed, time utility by making it available when needed, and possession utility by enabling acquisition. Prices signal scarcity, coordinate choices, and provide incentives.

> **Work through an example**
>
> You spend your only free hour volunteering instead of earning $16.
>
> The best foregone work opportunity is a $16 opportunity cost even though no invoice arrives.

## Distinguish movement and shift

A product’s own price change moves along its demand curve. Income, tastes, related-goods prices, and buyer numbers can shift demand. Technology, input costs, and seller numbers can shift supply. At equilibrium, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. A binding price ceiling below equilibrium tends to create shortage; a binding floor above equilibrium tends to create surplus.

## Compare systems and advantages

Market systems rely heavily on prices and private decisions; command systems use central direction. Actual economies mix institutions. Competition varies from many similar sellers to a monopoly with barriers to entry. Comparative advantage means lower opportunity cost, not simply faster production. Specialization and exchange can increase combined output. Elastic demand responds proportionately more than price; inelastic demand responds less. A private production cost can exclude an external cost imposed on neighbors, such as pollution.

Division of labor can improve productivity through specialization but can create coordination costs or narrow tasks. Diminishing marginal returns occur when adding more of one input to fixed other inputs eventually produces smaller extra output. A union represents workers in collective bargaining about employment terms. Negotiation and agreed procedures differ from assuming either side can obtain every requested outcome.

## Key terms to remember

**Comparative advantage.** Production at a lower opportunity cost.

**Equilibrium.** A price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
