## Find a customer need

Entrepreneurship combines opportunity recognition, resource coordination, and uncertainty. Start with a specific customer and problem. Interviews, observation, existing data, and small experiments reveal needs; leading questions and compliments do not prove demand. Distinguish a market trend from one anecdote. A value proposition explains why the intended customer would choose this solution over alternatives. Competitors include other ways of solving the problem, including doing nothing.

## Test the model and economics

A business model explains customers, value, channels, relationships, revenue, activities, resources, partners, and costs. A minimum viable test examines a risky assumption with limited resources; it is not permission to ignore safety or deceive customers. Estimate price, unit costs, demand, break-even, and cash timing. Debt requires repayment; equity exchanges an ownership claim. Resource planning includes skills, suppliers, technology, licenses, and working capital.

Compare buying, leasing, outsourcing, and partnering for needed resources. Calculate both money and coordination costs. Mentors, accountants, attorneys, community programs, and suppliers can fill expertise gaps, but the entrepreneur remains responsible for decisions. Hire when the expected value and workload justify the commitment rather than because every business appears to need a large staff.

> **Example**
>
> A club proposes a $10 lunch kit with $6 variable cost and $200 setup cost.
>
> Contribution is $4 per kit; break-even is 50 kits. Test willingness to buy before purchasing enough stock for 200.

## Launch and review the results

A plan should identify milestones, responsible people, legal requirements, risks, and evidence of progress. Choose an appropriate ownership form and protect intellectual property where relevant. Set purchasing, recordkeeping, customer-service, and quality procedures before scaling. Compare actual sales, costs, retention, and feedback with assumptions. A pivot changes an important model element in response to learning; random changes without evidence are not a strategy. Growth can increase cash needs even while sales rise.

## Key terms

**Value proposition.** The reason a target customer would choose an offering.

**Working capital.** Short-term resources needed to support ongoing operations.
