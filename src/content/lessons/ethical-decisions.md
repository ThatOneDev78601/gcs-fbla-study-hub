## Separate facts, pressure, and assumptions

Ethical decisions begin with accurate facts and the people affected. Identify what you know, what is uncertain, and whose interests are at risk. Notice pressure from loyalty, deadlines, incentives, or authority. Self-awareness means recognizing your own bias and limits, not merely feeling confident. Legal permission and company policy are relevant but do not answer every question about fairness, honesty, privacy, and harm.

Common biases include favoring familiar people, seeking only confirming evidence, and assuming a good result proves a good process. Ask what information would change your mind and whether you would defend the same decision if the affected people exchanged places.

## Compare alternatives openly

List feasible actions and compare consequences, duties, rights, fairness, and long-term trust. A conflict of interest arises when personal interests can interfere with responsibilities, even before misconduct happens. Disclose conflicts and follow the appropriate recusal or review process. A professional response explains the reasoning and evidence rather than attacking a person. Do not promise secrecy if reporting obligations may apply.

> **Example**
>
> You help choose a vendor and your cousin owns one bidder.
>
> Disclose the relationship before evaluation. Follow the organization’s conflict policy and let an independent decision-maker assess the bids; simply promising to be fair leaves the conflict unmanaged.

## Act, document, and learn

Choose a defensible action, identify who must approve it, communicate respectfully, and preserve relevant records. If a supervisor pressures you to falsify numbers, refuse the false entry and use the established escalation channel. Team members can disagree without retaliation. Invite constructive feedback, take responsibility for errors, and correct their effects. Career readiness includes reliability, respectful communication, confidentiality, and ethical use of technology. Afterward, examine whether incentives or weak processes helped create the problem.

Keep communication constructive: explain the issue, evidence, action, and owner. Accountability includes admitting an error and repairing its effects, not merely explaining why it happened. A useful team norm rewards early reporting of mistakes so they can be corrected before harm grows.

## Key terms

**Conflict of interest.** A personal interest that may interfere with a professional responsibility.

**Stakeholder.** A person or group affected by a decision.
