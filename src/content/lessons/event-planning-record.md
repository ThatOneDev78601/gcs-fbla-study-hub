## Plan the event

Begin with the purpose and measurable goals: "Host a family financial literacy night for 120 attendees that raises $1,000 for the chapter's scholarship fund." Define the audience, then build a backward timeline from the event date with every task, its owner, and its due date. Hold short planning meetings with an agenda and recorded action items.

Choose a venue using criteria such as capacity, location, accessibility, cost, parking, equipment, and availability of backup dates. Get written quotes from vendors (catering, audiovisual, rentals), compare at least two, and read contracts for deposits, cancellation terms, insurance requirements, and what happens in bad weather. Some details, such as permits, insurance, and food rules, depend on the venue and local government, so record what you must verify and with whom.

## Budget forecasts and actuals

A budget lists expected income (tickets, sponsorships, donations) and expenses. In-kind support, such as donated food or a free venue, should be listed separately at its fair value so readers see the event's true cost without inflating cash. Include a contingency of about 10% for surprises. After the event, record actual figures and calculate the variance for each line.

| Line | Forecast | Actual | Variance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ticket income (120 x $10) | $1,200 | $1,040 (104 tickets) | $160 unfavorable |
| Sponsorships | $500 | $650 | $150 favorable |
| Food | ($450) | ($410) | $40 favorable |
| Supplies and printing | ($150) | ($185) | $35 unfavorable |
| Contingency | ($100) | $0 used | $100 favorable |
| Net | $1,000 | $1,095 | $95 favorable |
| In-kind: venue donated by school (fair value) | $400 | $400 | Not cash; reported separately |

- Explain each large variance: attendance fell short because of a schedule conflict, but an added sponsor covered the gap.
- Keep every receipt and deposit record; the report's cost claims should match them.

## Layout, staffing, safety, and backups

Draw a floor plan showing entrances, registration, seating, stations, accessible routes, and emergency exits, and stay within the venue's posted capacity. Assign staff and volunteers to roles with a shift schedule and a briefing sheet. Plan safety: first aid, emergency contacts, a lost-child procedure for family events, and food allergy labeling. Write backup plans for likely problems such as rain, a speaker canceling, technology failure, or low attendance.

## Promote, run the day, and report

Promotion follows the audience: flyers through schools, social posts, partner newsletters, and personal invitations, each with a tracking method such as a registration source question. On the event day, use a run sheet with times and owners, track attendance with sign-in or ticket scans, keep an incident log, and record any unexpected problem and how it was solved.

Collect feedback with a short survey (three to five questions, including one open question). The final report and presentation show evidence of execution (photos with permission, attendance records), attendee satisfaction results, financial results against the budget, what went well, what went wrong, and specific improvements for next time.

> **Example**
>
> Thirty minutes before the event, the projector fails.
>
> The tech lead switches to the backup plan: the presentation loaded on a second laptop connected to the gym's TV cart, plus printed handouts of key slides. The incident log records the time, the problem, the response, and a lesson: test the venue projector the day before. Reporting this honestly shows judges real problem-solving.

> **Try it yourself**
>
> Forecast: 200 tickets at $8 and $300 of sponsorships; expenses of $600 for food and $250 for supplies. Actual: 176 tickets, $450 of sponsorships, food $540, supplies $270. Calculate forecast net, actual net, and the variance, and write one sentence explaining it.

## Key terms

**Backward timeline.** A schedule built from the event date back to the present.

**In-kind donation.** Goods or services donated instead of cash, recorded at fair value.

**Contingency.** Budget set aside for unexpected costs.

**Run sheet.** A timed list of event-day tasks and owners.

**Budget variance.** The difference between a forecast and the actual amount.
