## Which document says what

This lesson summarizes the FBLA High School Division Bylaws as amended June 26, 2023, the version linked from FBLA's website when this site checked its sources. The division bylaws were first adopted July 1, 1969. The corporate bylaws govern the whole organization, and each division has its own bylaws. Before a test, check fbla.org for a newer amendment date, and use the official text for any exact wording.

Article XII makes Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised the parliamentary authority wherever it applies and does not conflict with FBLA's rules, bylaws, or special rules of order. Article XI names blue and gold as the official colors and states that the emblem is a registered trademark.

## Purpose, goals, and membership

Article II states the division's purpose: to give secondary students in business and business-related fields extra opportunities to develop career competencies and to promote civic and personal responsibility, as an integral part of the instructional program. It then lists nine goals. Grouped by theme, they cover leadership and confidence, understanding American business enterprise, projects that improve home, business, and community, character and citizenship, efficient money management, scholarship and school loyalty, occupational goals, and the transition from school to work. Learn the official list word for word from the bylaws if your test requires exact wording.

Article III sets membership classes. Active members are secondary students in business or business-related fields who accept FBLA's purpose, subscribe to its creed, and pay dues; they may compete, serve as voting delegates, and hold office. Honorary life members are elected by a state or local chapter by majority vote for helping business education or FBLA; they do not vote, hold office, or pay dues. National honorary life members are recommended by the membership and approved by the board of directors.

Article IV sets the fiscal year as August 1 through July 31. National dues are set by majority vote of the national board of directors.

## Organization and officers

National officers serve one year, beginning at the close of the National Leadership Conference where they were selected. A candidate must be an active member present at the NLC, certified by the officer screening committee, hold or have held a local or state office, have at least a full year left in a business program, and be endorsed by local and state chapters. If the presidency becomes vacant, the vice president from the president's region becomes president. Other vacancies are filled by presidential appointment with executive council approval.

| Body or officer | Role under the division bylaws |
| --- | --- |
| Board of directors | Policymaking body; manages FBLA's affairs and property; members serve three-year terms |
| Association president and CEO | Administers FBLA and its finances; submits the annual budget and audit |
| National executive council | The division's national officers plus ex-officio, nonvoting members; recommends to the board and reviews proposed amendments |
| State chapter | Needs at least five local chapters and an annual meeting to qualify for a charter |
| Local chapter | Must have a faculty adviser |
| National president | Chairs the executive council, presides at business meetings, sits on the board, appoints committees and the parliamentarian, and is an ex-officio, nonvoting member of all committees |
| Five regional vice presidents | Eastern, Mountain Plains, North Central, Southern, and Western; assist the president and preside at regional meetings at the NLC |
| National secretary | Keeps records of business meetings and the executive council |
| National treasurer | Helps track officer travel expenses and presents an annual financial report at the NLC |
| National parliamentarian | Appointed, not elected; advises the president on orderly procedure |

## The National Leadership Conference and amendments

Article VIII requires a National Leadership Conference every year. Each state chapter in good standing may send two voting delegates and one alternate. Each local chapter may send voting delegates based on size: two for under 50 members, three for 50 to 100, and four for over 100, with one alternate per delegate. State delegates vote in the general session; local delegates vote in regional meetings. Proxy voting is not allowed. The quorum is a majority of registered voting delegates.

Under Article XIII, proposed amendments are submitted in writing by local or state chapters or a national officer by April 1, reviewed by the Governance Committee, and sent to states by May 15. Adoption requires a two-thirds vote of the state voting delegates present and voting at the NLC.

## Key terms

**Active member.** A dues-paying student member who may compete, vote as a delegate, and hold office.

**Honorary life member.** A non-student recognized for service to FBLA who does not vote, hold office, or pay dues.

**National executive council.** The division's national officers plus ex-officio members who recommend policy and review amendments.

**Voting delegate.** A certified member who votes for a chapter at the National Leadership Conference.

**Proxy voting.** Voting on behalf of an absent person, which FBLA's division bylaws prohibit at the NLC.
