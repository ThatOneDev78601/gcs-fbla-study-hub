## FBLA’s main milestones

Hamden L. Forkner developed the FBLA concept in 1937. The first high-school chapter was chartered at Science Hill High School in Johnson City, Tennessee, in 1942. FBLA Collegiate developed from Phi Beta Lambda; the organization unified its student divisions under the FBLA name in 2022. Study the sequence and meaning instead of treating every old handout as current.

## Purpose and values

FBLA connects career preparation, leadership, and community responsibility. The mission expresses its purpose, goals describe intended outcomes, the pledge is a commitment, and the creed expresses beliefs. Study the exact current wording in the official source separately; a paraphrase is useful for understanding but is not a quotation to memorize. Connect each value to a practical behavior such as accurate work, service, respectful collaboration, or responsible money management.

Group the goals by theme: leadership and confidence, understanding business, community contribution and citizenship, responsible money management, scholarship, career planning, and the transition to work. These themes help you reconstruct meaning without replacing the exact official wording. For a scenario question, connect the conduct to a theme: a well-documented service project develops responsibility and community contribution; budgeting a fundraiser practices money management.

## Create an FBLA study sheet

Organize your notes under history, structure, governing documents, programs, policies, publications, communications, and deadlines. Write each fact with its source and date. Use recall prompts such as “Who, where, when, and why?” rather than rereading passively. Keep changing details on a separate current-season card. The Introduction to FBLA guide supplies knowledge areas but no test composition, so this site does not invent topic weights. Current officers, partners, publications, and deadlines must be checked against official pages before a conference.
