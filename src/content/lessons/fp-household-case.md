## Gather and organize the facts

This lesson uses the fictional Rivera household to show the method. It is not the competition case. Start by sorting every fact into a table before making any recommendation. Mark facts that are missing and state the assumption you will use instead.

The Riveras are a married couple, ages 38 and 36, with two children ages 6 and 10. They take home $7,400 a month. They have $4,000 in checking and savings, $52,000 in workplace retirement accounts, a home worth $340,000 with a $255,000 mortgage, a car worth $14,000 with a $9,000 loan, and $11,000 of credit card debt across two cards. One spouse has employer life insurance equal to one year of salary; the other has none. They want to rebuild savings after a job loss, pay off the cards, and start saving for college.

| Category | Facts | Missing or assumed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income | $7,400 a month take-home | Gross pay and tax withholding not given |
| Spending | Mortgage $1,850; car $300; minimums $330; other essentials $3,400; discretionary $900 | Assume spending is accurate; ask for 3 months of statements |
| Assets | Cash $4,000; retirement $52,000; home $340,000; car $14,000 | Retirement asset allocation not given |
| Debts | Mortgage $255,000 at 6%; car $9,000 at 7%; Card A $8,000 at 24%; Card B $3,000 at 18% | Card minimums assumed at $240 and $90 |
| Protection | Employer life on one spouse only; no disability coverage listed | Check employer disability benefits |
| Goals | Emergency fund, eliminate card debt, college saving | Ask for target dates and amounts |

## Measure the starting position

Build a personal balance sheet and a monthly cash flow statement, then compute a few standard measures. Use them to set priorities, not to judge the family.

1. Net worth: assets 4,000 + 52,000 + 340,000 + 14,000 = 410,000; liabilities 255,000 + 9,000 + 11,000 = 275,000; net worth $135,000.
2. Monthly surplus: 7,400 - (1,850 + 300 + 330 + 3,400 + 900) = 7,400 - 6,780 = $620.
3. Essential monthly expenses: 1,850 + 300 + 330 + 3,400 = $5,880.
4. Emergency fund coverage: 4,000 / 5,880 = 0.7 months, below the common 3 to 6 month guideline.
5. Credit card interest: 8,000 x 24% / 12 + 3,000 x 18% / 12 = 160 + 45 = about $205 a month.

## Compare alternatives for each goal

For each goal, list at least two options with their costs, risks, and tradeoffs. The debt avalanche pays extra on the highest-rate debt first and costs the least interest. The debt snowball pays the smallest balance first and gives a quicker win, which helps some families stay motivated. Here the avalanche targets Card A (24%) and the snowball targets Card B ($3,000).

| Goal | Option | Advantage | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency fund | Keep $1,000 starter fund, then build after cards | Cards cost 24% interest | Thin cushion for several months |
| Emergency fund | Build to one month first | Safer if income is uncertain | Pays more card interest meanwhile |
| Card debt | Avalanche (Card A first) | Least total interest | Slower first payoff |
| Card debt | 0% balance transfer card | Could save most interest for a set period | Transfer fee, often 3% to 5%; rate jumps afterward; requires good credit |
| College | 529 plan | Tax-free growth for qualified education costs | Penalty and tax on earnings for nonqualified withdrawals |
| College | Taxable brokerage account | Flexible use | Taxes on gains |
| Protection | Term life for both spouses | Low cost while children are dependent | No cash value |

- Retirement: do not reduce contributions below the level that earns the full employer match, because the match is an immediate return.
- State assumptions with dates, for example: rates as of November 2026, a 6% assumed long-term return, and 3% inflation.

## Write and present the recommendations

Write each recommendation as: the goal, the action, the numbers, the reason, and when to review it. Then show how the recommendations fit together in one monthly budget so they do not spend the same $620 twice.

Say when a qualified professional is needed. An attorney drafts wills and guardianship documents, a tax professional handles complex returns, and a licensed insurance agent or fee-only planner can price specific policies. Name review triggers such as a job change, a new child, a large raise, a move, or a major market drop.

> **Example**
>
> Show one complete recommendation for the Riveras' card debt.
>
> Goal: eliminate $11,000 of card debt. Action: keep paying all minimums, cut discretionary spending by $200, and send the $620 surplus plus $200 to Card A until it is paid, then roll that amount to Card B. Numbers: about $1,150 a month goes to the cards (the $330 of minimums plus $820 extra), which clears both cards in about 11 months if no new charges are made. Reason: Card A's 24% rate is the costliest debt. Review: monthly, and stop new card charges. If credit allows, compare a balance transfer including its fee.

> **Try it yourself**
>

## Key terms

**Personal cash flow statement.** A summary of a household's income and spending over a period.

**Debt avalanche.** Paying extra toward the highest-interest debt first.

**Debt snowball.** Paying extra toward the smallest balance first.

**Review trigger.** A life or market event that should prompt a plan update.

**529 plan.** A state-sponsored education savings account with tax-free growth for qualified costs.
