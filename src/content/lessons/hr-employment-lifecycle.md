## Plan the workforce and define the job

Workforce planning compares the people the business will need, based on growth plans and turnover, with the people it will have. Gaps are filled by hiring, training, promotion, or contractors; surpluses by attrition, transfers, or layoffs.

Job analysis studies what a job requires. It produces a job description, listing duties, responsibilities, and working conditions, and a job specification, listing the knowledge, skills, abilities, and qualifications needed. Accurate job descriptions support fair hiring, pay decisions, and ADA accommodation decisions, since they identify essential functions.

## Recruit and select

Internal recruiting promotes or transfers current employees, which rewards loyalty and costs less. External recruiting uses job boards, referrals, campus events, and agencies, and brings new skills. An applicant tracking system stores and screens applications, but keyword filters can reject qualified people, so review the criteria.

Structured interviews ask every candidate the same job-related questions and score answers with a rubric, which is more reliable and fairer than casual conversation. Behavioral questions ask about past actions: "Tell me about a time you handled an upset customer." Situational questions ask what the candidate would do. Selection tests must be valid, meaning they predict job performance.

Do not ask about protected characteristics such as age, religion, national origin, marital or family status, pregnancy, or disability. You may ask whether a candidate can perform the essential functions with or without accommodation, and whether they are authorized to work in the United States. Background checks through a consumer reporting agency require written consent under the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

- Form I-9 verifies identity and work authorization. The employee completes Section 1 by the first day; the employer completes Section 2 within three business days of the start date.
- Form W-4 sets federal income tax withholding.
- New-hire records also include the offer letter, emergency contacts, benefits elections, and state new-hire reporting.

## Onboard, train, and manage performance

Orientation covers policies, safety, benefits, and introductions. Onboarding is longer, often 90 days or more, and helps a new employee become productive and connected. Training follows the ADDIE model: analyze needs, design objectives, develop materials, implement, and evaluate. Methods include on-the-job training, job shadowing, mentoring, simulations, and e-learning. Supervisors need extra training in delegation, feedback, documentation, and legal responsibilities.

Kirkpatrick's four levels evaluate training: reaction (did they like it), learning (did they gain knowledge), behavior (do they use it on the job), and results (did business outcomes improve).

Performance management sets goals, gives ongoing feedback and coaching, and conducts formal reviews. Appraisal methods include graphic rating scales, behaviorally anchored rating scales (BARS), management by objectives, and 360-degree feedback from managers, peers, and reports. Rater errors include the halo effect (one trait colors all ratings), recency (only recent events count), leniency, and central tendency (rating everyone average). Team evaluation measures shared goals and how members contribute to them.

## Pay, benefits, and total rewards

Total rewards include compensation, benefits, well-being programs, recognition, and development. Compensation structures group jobs into pay grades with ranges, based on job evaluation and market surveys. Employers must follow the Fair Labor Standards Act's minimum wage and overtime rules, the Equal Pay Act, and state laws.

Legally required benefits include employer contributions to Social Security and Medicare, unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, and job-protected FMLA leave for eligible employees. Under the Affordable Care Act, employers with 50 or more full-time equivalent employees must offer affordable coverage or may owe a penalty. COBRA lets employees of employers with 20 or more employees continue group health coverage, usually for up to 18 months, by paying the premium. Voluntary benefits include health insurance at smaller employers, retirement plans, paid time off, and tuition assistance.

> **Example**
>
> An employee is offered either $58,000 with no retirement match, or $55,000 with a 5% 401(k) match she will fully use. Her health premiums are the same.
>
> The second offer's match is 5% x 55,000 = $2,750, so total compensation is $57,750, close to the first offer. Retirement money also grows tax-deferred, while the first offer's extra $3,000 is taxable now. A full total rewards comparison also weighs paid time off, flexibility, and growth.

## Employee relations, safety, and exits

A complaint or grievance should be handled through a written procedure: receive it, investigate promptly and impartially, document interviews and evidence, decide, communicate the outcome, and protect the employee from retaliation. In a unionized workplace, the collective bargaining agreement sets grievance steps, often ending in arbitration. The National Labor Relations Act protects employees' right to organize and to discuss pay and working conditions; the National Labor Relations Board enforces it.

Progressive discipline escalates from a documented verbal warning to a written warning, then suspension, then termination, unless the misconduct is serious enough to skip steps. A termination meeting should be brief, private, factual, and respectful, with a witness and final pay information. An exit interview with departing employees gathers honest feedback about why people leave.

OSHA's general duty clause requires a workplace free of recognized serious hazards. Employers must report a work-related fatality within 8 hours and an inpatient hospitalization, amputation, or eye loss within 24 hours. Many employers with more than 10 employees must keep an injury and illness log. An incident response includes first aid, securing the area, reporting, investigating root causes, and correcting the hazard.

HR technology includes human resources information systems (HRIS), applicant tracking, payroll, and learning management systems; they hold sensitive data, so access must be limited. HR careers include recruiter, generalist, compensation analyst, trainer, and HR manager. Certifications include the SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP from the Society for Human Resource Management and the aPHR, PHR, and SPHR from HRCI.

## Key terms

**Job specification.** The qualifications, skills, and abilities a job requires.

**Structured interview.** An interview using the same job-related questions and scoring for every candidate.

**Total rewards.** Everything of value an employer provides: pay, benefits, well-being, recognition, and development.

**Progressive discipline.** Discipline that increases in severity with repeated problems.

**Halo effect.** A rating error in which one strong trait influences all other ratings.

**COBRA.** A law letting departing employees continue group health coverage at their own cost.
