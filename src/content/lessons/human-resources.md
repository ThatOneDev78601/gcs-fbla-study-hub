## Start with the job

Human-resource planning compares needed capabilities with available people. A job analysis identifies tasks and requirements; a job description explains duties, while a job specification describes needed qualifications. Recruit through relevant channels, evaluate consistently, and document job-related evidence. Structured interviews ask comparable questions, and selection tools should measure useful requirements rather than irrelevant personal characteristics. Onboarding introduces both the work and the organization’s expectations.

## Develop and evaluate fairly

Training closes specific skill gaps; development prepares people for broader future roles. Determine the gap before choosing a course, provide practice, and evaluate whether behavior or performance improves. Performance management is an ongoing cycle of clear expectations, feedback, evidence, and support. Avoid halo effects, recency bias, and comparisons based only on personality. An improvement plan should identify the gap, resources, actions, measures, and follow-up date.

Evaluate both individual and team outcomes. Attendance, task quality, collaboration, and customer impact need evidence and context. A grievance process should receive, investigate, document, and resolve a complaint fairly; serious harassment or violence concerns require prompt escalation through the designated safety or HR process. Separation should follow applicable rules, authorization, documentation, and respectful communication; an exit interview can reveal process problems.

> **Example**
>
> A new employee repeatedly enters purchase orders incorrectly.
>
> Check whether the procedure and training are clear, demonstrate the correct process, provide guided practice, and review error rates afterward instead of immediately assuming poor motivation.

## Support the employment relationship

Total rewards include pay, benefits, recognition, growth, and working conditions. Internal equity concerns fairness among jobs; external competitiveness compares relevant labor markets. Employee relations includes listening, grievances, conflict resolution, and respectful labor-management communication. Safety requires hazard identification, prevention, training, and reporting rather than merely reacting after injury. Protect personnel data and limit access. HR careers may specialize in recruitment, compensation, learning, employee relations, or organizational development.

Payroll administration tracks time, calculates gross pay and deductions, pays employees, and remits amounts due. Benefit plans can include health and life coverage, educational assistance, and retirement support. Emergency plans identify roles, communication, evacuation or protective actions, and practice. Professional development can include PHR, SPHR, or GPHR certifications associated with operational, strategic, or global HR work; verify current credential requirements with the issuing organization.

## Key terms

**Job analysis.** Systematic study of tasks and job requirements.

**Total rewards.** The combination of pay, benefits, recognition, and other work-related value.
