## Auto and home

Auto liability coverage pays others when the insured driver is at fault. Split limits such as 50/100/25 mean $50,000 per injured person, $100,000 total for injuries in one accident, and $25,000 for property damage. Each state sets minimum limits, and Maryland also requires personal injury protection and uninsured motorist coverage. Collision pays for damage to the insured car from a crash. Comprehensive pays for other causes such as theft, hail, fire, or hitting a deer. Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage protects the insured when the at-fault driver lacks enough insurance.

Homeowners forms are named HO-1 through HO-8. The HO-3 is the most common policy for owner-occupied houses. The HO-4 is renters insurance, which covers belongings and liability but not the building. The HO-6 covers condominium owners. A homeowners policy has standard sections: Coverage A for the dwelling, B for other structures, C for personal property, D for loss of use, E for personal liability, and F for medical payments to others. Floods and earthquakes are usually excluded. Flood coverage is available through the National Flood Insurance Program and private insurers.

> **Example**
>
> A driver with 50/100/25 limits causes a crash. One person has $70,000 of injuries, a second has $20,000, and the other car has $30,000 of damage.
>
> Bodily injury: the policy pays $50,000 for the first person (the per-person limit) and $20,000 for the second, totaling $70,000, under the $100,000 accident limit. Property damage: it pays $25,000, the limit. The driver is personally responsible for the remaining $20,000 of injury costs and $5,000 of property damage.

## Health, life, and disability

A health deductible is the amount the insured pays before the plan shares costs. Coinsurance is the insured's percentage after the deductible. A copay is a flat fee for a service. The out-of-pocket maximum caps what the insured pays in a year for covered care. An HMO requires in-network care and usually a primary care referral. A PPO allows out-of-network care at higher cost.

Term life insurance covers a set period, has no cash value, and costs the least for young buyers. Whole life covers the insured's lifetime and builds cash value at a guaranteed rate. Universal life has flexible premiums and death benefits. Variable life invests cash value in subaccounts, so its value can fall. Disability insurance replaces part of income when illness or injury prevents work. The elimination period is the waiting time before benefits start.

Medicare is federal health insurance mainly for people 65 and older and some younger people with disabilities. Part A covers hospital care, Part B covers doctor and outpatient care, Part C (Medicare Advantage) is private plans that replace A and B, and Part D covers prescription drugs. Medicaid is a joint federal and state program based on financial need, and each state runs its own version.

> **Example**
>
> A member has a $1,000 deductible, 20% coinsurance, and a $4,000 out-of-pocket maximum. A covered hospital bill is $3,000.
>
> The member pays the first $1,000, then 20% of the remaining $2,000, which is $400. The member's total is $1,400 and the plan pays $1,600. The member is still well under the $4,000 maximum.

## Liability and commercial lines

Unemployment insurance is a government program run by each state and funded mainly by employer payroll taxes. Disaster coverage combines several sources: property policies, flood and earthquake coverage, and federal assistance. International businesses buy political risk insurance and coverage for foreign operations. Farms use federal crop insurance through the USDA Risk Management Agency. Newer products include usage-based auto insurance that prices by measured driving, parametric insurance that pays a set amount when an event such as a wind speed occurs, and on-demand coverage bought for a single trip or item.

| Coverage | Protects against |
| --- | --- |
| Personal umbrella | Liability above auto and home limits |
| Commercial property | Damage to business buildings, equipment, and inventory |
| Business interruption | Lost income while a covered loss closes the business |
| Commercial general liability (CGL) | Injuries and property damage to others from business operations or premises |
| Business owner's policy (BOP) | A package of property and liability coverage for small businesses |
| Professional liability (errors and omissions, malpractice) | Claims of negligent professional services |
| Directors and officers (D&O) | Claims against leaders for management decisions |
| Product liability | Injuries caused by products the business makes or sells |
| Cyber insurance | Data breach response, ransomware, and related liability |
| Workers' compensation | Medical care and lost wages for work-related injuries, regardless of fault; required by state law |
| Title insurance | Losses from defects in a property's past ownership record; paid once at closing |
| Inland and ocean marine | Goods in transit and movable property |
| Surety bond | Guarantees that a party will fulfill a contract; the surety may seek repayment from that party |

## Match coverage to a need

Start with what could be lost: property, income, health, or the ability to pay a lawsuit. Then name the coverage that responds and the gaps that remain.

> **Try it yourself**
>
> A family rents an apartment, owns one car with a loan, has a new baby, and one parent runs a small online design business from home. List the coverage they should review and one gap for each.

## Key terms

**Split limits.** Separate auto liability limits per person, per accident, and for property damage.

**Coinsurance (health).** The share of covered costs the insured pays after the deductible.

**Term life insurance.** Life insurance for a set period with no cash value.

**Elimination period.** The waiting time before disability benefits begin.

**Workers' compensation.** State-required, no-fault coverage for employees' work-related injuries.
