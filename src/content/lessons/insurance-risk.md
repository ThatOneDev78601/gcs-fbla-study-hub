## Identify and treat risk

Risk management identifies exposures, assesses likelihood and severity, selects treatment, and reviews results. Avoidance stops an activity, reduction lowers probability or severity, retention accepts a loss exposure, and transfer shifts financial consequences through arrangements such as insurance. Pure risk involves loss or no loss; speculative risk also includes gain. Insurance pools eligible losses, but it does not eliminate the underlying hazard.

Enterprise risk management considers connected exposures across the whole organization, including operations, finance, reputation, and technology. Self-insurance retains planned exposure, a captive is an insurer established for its owners’ risks, and reinsurance transfers an insurer’s risk to another insurer. Analytics can reveal patterns but must protect privacy and account for data quality.

## Read the coverage structure

A premium is the price of coverage. A deductible is the insured’s specified share of covered loss; a limit caps payment, and exclusions identify situations outside coverage. Life insurance addresses a covered death; health covers defined medical expenses; property covers specified damage; liability addresses covered claims against the insured. Commercial exposures may require business property, liability, workers’ compensation, professional liability, or business-interruption coverage. Actual payment depends on all policy terms.

Auto coverage can include liability, collision, and comprehensive protections with different triggers. Homeowners and renters policies address different property interests; renters need coverage for their own belongings. Flood and earthquake losses may require separate coverage. Disability coverage concerns lost earning ability; title insurance addresses specified title defects. Workers’ compensation addresses covered work-related injuries, while unemployment insurance serves a different income-support purpose. Medicare and Medicaid are different public health programs with distinct eligibility and administration.

> **Example**
>
> A covered property loss is $3,000, with a $500 deductible and an adequate limit, assuming no other policy adjustment.
>
> The insurer pays $2,500 and the insured bears $500. Do not subtract the deductible from an uncovered loss and assume it becomes covered.

## Underwriting, claims, and integrity

Underwriters evaluate risk and eligibility for terms; actuaries analyze patterns to support pricing and reserves; claims staff investigate and resolve reported losses. Insurable interest and accurate disclosure help distinguish insurance from a wager. Indemnity generally aims to compensate covered loss rather than create a profit. Preserve evidence and report claims honestly. Fraud can involve fabricated losses or misrepresented facts. Licensed producers explain products, while adjusters handle claims; state regulation and professional ethics support fair treatment.

A claim workflow identifies the policy and date, documents circumstances, evaluates coverage, verifies evidence, values the covered loss, and communicates the decision. Subrogation can allow an insurer that paid a loss to pursue the responsible party under applicable rights. Claims analytics can flag anomalies but a flag is not proof of fraud. Professional licensing requirements vary by role and jurisdiction; continuing education and ethical conduct matter after the initial credential.

## Key terms

**Deductible.** The insured’s specified share of a covered loss.

**Underwriting.** Evaluating risks and terms for accepting coverage.
