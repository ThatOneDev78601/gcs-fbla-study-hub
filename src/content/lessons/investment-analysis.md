## Know the claim you are buying

A stock represents ownership; a bond represents debt. Bond investors face default and interest-rate risk; existing fixed-rate bond prices generally move opposite market interest rates. Mutual funds and ETFs pool investments but differ in trading mechanics and terms. A primary market raises funds through new securities; secondary markets trade existing ones. Market orders prioritize execution at available prices; limit orders specify an acceptable price and may not execute.

A margin account permits borrowing against securities and can magnify losses as well as gains. Short selling involves selling borrowed securities with an obligation to return them; a rising price can create large losses. Investment banks help issuers raise capital in primary offerings. Interest rates, earnings expectations, economic conditions, and new information influence security prices.

## Calculate and compare

Total holding-period return includes price change and income relative to initial cost. Compare fees, time horizon, liquidity, diversification, and risk rather than chasing the highest recent return. Diversification can reduce concentration risk but cannot eliminate all losses. A portfolio’s allocation should match goals and ability to bear uncertainty. Fundamental analysis examines business and financial evidence; technical analysis studies price and volume patterns. Neither guarantees prediction.

> **Example**
>
> Buy a share for $50, receive a $2 dividend, then sell for $53, ignoring fees and taxes.
>
> Total return is ($53 − $50 + $2) / $50 = 10%. Counting only the price rise would omit the dividend.

## Research the industry and avoid deception

Read official disclosures, financial statements, prospectuses, and fee schedules. Understand the role of brokers, investment advisers, issuers, exchanges, and regulators. Verify professionals through official tools and be wary of guaranteed high returns or pressure to act immediately. Money’s time value means the timing of cash flows affects their value; inflation reduces real purchasing power. This module teaches exam concepts rather than recommending a product, trade, or allocation.

Retirement and education accounts are account structures with applicable tax and withdrawal rules, not investments that automatically earn a return. A derivative derives value from an underlying item; an option gives specified rights, while futures establish contractual obligations. Commodity, real-estate, venture-capital, and hedge-fund exposures have different liquidity and risk. Variable annuities combine insurance features with investment exposure and can involve significant fees or restrictions. Market timing attempts to predict entry and exit points; past success does not establish reliable future prediction.

## Key terms

**Bond.** A debt security representing an issuer’s borrowing obligation.

**Diversification.** Spreading exposure across different investments.
