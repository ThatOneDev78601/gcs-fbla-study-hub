## Agency

In an agency relationship, an agent acts on behalf of a principal. Agency can be created by express agreement, by implication from conduct, or by ratification when a principal approves an unauthorized act afterward. An agent has actual authority (express or implied) when the principal grants it, and apparent authority when the principal's conduct leads a third party to reasonably believe the agent has authority.

An agent owes the principal duties of loyalty (no secret profits or competing), obedience, reasonable care, accounting for money, and notification of important information. The principal owes the agent compensation, reimbursement of proper expenses, indemnification, and cooperation.

Under respondeat superior, an employer is liable for torts its employees commit within the scope of employment. A business is generally not liable for the torts of independent contractors, so the distinction matters. Signs of employee status include the employer controlling how and when work is done, providing tools, and paying regularly.

## Debtors, creditors, and the UCC

The Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), adopted in some form by every state, governs commercial transactions. Article 2 covers the sale of goods, Article 3 covers negotiable instruments such as checks and notes, and Article 9 covers secured transactions.

A secured creditor holds a security interest in specific collateral. The interest attaches when there is a security agreement, the creditor gives value, and the debtor has rights in the collateral. The creditor usually perfects the interest by filing a financing statement with the state, which gives priority over later creditors. An unsecured creditor has only a general claim.

Other creditor remedies include liens (a mechanic's lien for work on real property, an artisan's lien for repairs to personal property, and a judgment lien) and garnishment, a court order directing a third party such as an employer to pay part of the debtor's wages to the creditor. A guarantor pays only if the debtor fails and the creditor first tries to collect from the debtor; a surety is directly liable from the start.

Bankruptcy is federal law. Chapter 7 liquidates nonexempt assets to pay creditors and discharges most remaining debts. Chapter 11 lets a business reorganize and keep operating. Chapter 13 lets individuals with regular income keep property under a three-to-five-year repayment plan. Filing creates an automatic stay that stops most collection actions.

## Intellectual property

Ideas and facts alone are not copyrightable. A business chooses protection based on the asset. A patent requires public disclosure, so some companies keep processes as trade secrets instead. Infringement remedies include injunctions and damages.

| Type | Protects | Length | Registered with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Utility patent | New, useful, non-obvious inventions and processes | 20 years from filing | U.S. Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) |
| Design patent | New ornamental design of a product | 15 years from grant | USPTO |
| Trademark | Words, logos, and symbols that identify a source | As long as it is used; registrations are renewed | USPTO (rights also come from use) |
| Copyright | Original creative works fixed in a tangible form | Author's life plus 70 years; works made for hire 95 years from publication | U.S. Copyright Office (registration is optional but needed before suing for a U.S. work) |
| Trade secret | Valuable information kept confidential, such as a recipe | As long as it stays secret | Not registered; protected by reasonable secrecy measures |

## Environmental and consumer protection

The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) enforces most federal environmental laws. The National Environmental Policy Act requires environmental impact statements for major federal actions. The Clean Air Act and Clean Water Act set pollution limits. The Resource Conservation and Recovery Act regulates hazardous waste from creation to disposal. CERCLA, the Superfund law, can make current and past owners of contaminated sites liable for cleanup even if they did not cause the pollution.

The Federal Trade Commission Act bans unfair or deceptive acts and practices. The Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act sets rules for written consumer warranties. The Consumer Product Safety Commission sets safety standards and orders recalls. The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act limits how third-party debt collectors may act, such as banning harassment and calls at unusual hours. The Fair Credit Billing Act lets consumers dispute billing errors on credit cards. The FTC's Cooling-Off Rule gives buyers three days to cancel certain sales made at the buyer's home or at temporary locations. State lemon laws give remedies for new cars with repeated defects.

## Employment, financial, and tax law

Most U.S. employment, including in Maryland, is at will: either side may end it at any time for any lawful reason. Exceptions include contracts, discrimination laws, retaliation for protected activity, and firings that violate public policy, such as firing someone for serving on a jury.

Financial law includes securities laws, the Sarbanes-Oxley Act's reporting and audit rules, the Dodd-Frank Act, and consumer credit laws such as the Truth in Lending Act and the Credit CARD Act of 2009. Taxes include income taxes (progressive, rising with income), payroll taxes, sales taxes (regressive, taking a larger share of a low income), excise taxes on specific goods, property taxes, estate taxes, and tariffs. Tax avoidance uses legal methods to lower taxes. Tax evasion, such as hiding income, is a crime.

> **Example**
>
> A software startup wants to protect its app name, its source code, and a new data compression method.
>
> Register the app name as a trademark. The source code is automatically copyrighted when written; registering it allows a lawsuit and statutory damages. The compression method may be patentable if it is new and non-obvious, or it could be kept as a trade secret if it can stay hidden from users.

## Key terms

**Respondeat superior.** The rule that an employer is liable for employees' torts within the scope of employment.

**Apparent authority.** Authority a third party reasonably believes an agent has because of the principal's conduct.

**Perfection.** Steps, usually filing a financing statement, that give a secured creditor priority over others.

**Automatic stay.** A bankruptcy rule that halts most collection actions once a petition is filed.

**Trade secret.** Confidential business information that gives a competitive advantage.

**Employment at will.** Employment that either party may end at any time for any lawful reason.
