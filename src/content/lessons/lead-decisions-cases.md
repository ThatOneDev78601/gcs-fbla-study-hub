## Styles and sources of power

Leadership styles fit different situations. Autocratic leaders decide alone, which suits emergencies. Democratic leaders involve the team, which builds commitment. Laissez-faire leaders give wide freedom, which suits experts. Transactional leaders use rewards for meeting goals. Transformational leaders inspire people around a shared vision. Servant leaders put team members' growth first. Situational leadership adjusts direction and support to each person's skill and commitment.

French and Raven identified five bases of power. Legitimate power comes from position, reward power from the ability to give rewards, coercive power from the ability to punish, expert power from knowledge, and referent power from being respected and liked. Expert and referent power last longer and produce willing commitment; coercive power produces compliance at best. Appropriate power fits the situation and is used ethically.

Emotional intelligence includes self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, and social skill. Leaders with high emotional intelligence notice their own reactions and others' feelings and respond deliberately.

## Lead change and shape culture

Organizational culture is the shared values, beliefs, and habits that shape how work actually gets done. Leaders shape it by what they pay attention to, reward, and model, more than by posters. Adaptability means adjusting plans when conditions change while keeping core values.

People resist change because of fear of loss, uncertainty, habit, or distrust. Reduce resistance by explaining why the change is needed, involving people in planning, training them, and celebrating early wins. Kotter's approach starts with creating urgency, building a guiding coalition, and communicating a vision, and ends by anchoring the change in the culture.

> **Example**
>
> A clinic is switching from paper scheduling to online booking. Long-time front-desk staff are worried.
>
> The manager explains the reason: patients have complained about phone wait times, and missed appointments are rising. Two respected front-desk staff help test the system and become trainers (building a coalition and using referent power). Training happens before launch, the old system runs in parallel for two weeks, and the manager shares the first month's drop in phone wait times. Feedback leads to adjustments, which shows the change is shared rather than imposed.

## Decide, create, and manage time

A rational decision process defines the problem, gathers information, generates alternatives, evaluates them against criteria, chooses, implements, and reviews results. Groups add ideas and buy-in but risk groupthink, where the wish for agreement suppresses doubt. Prevent it by assigning a devil's advocate, inviting outside views, and having members write ideas before discussion.

Creativity produces new ideas; innovation puts them to use. Brainstorming separates generating ideas from judging them. Problem-solving tools include the five whys for finding root causes and a fishbone diagram for sorting possible causes.

For time management, the Eisenhower matrix sorts tasks by urgent and important: do urgent-important tasks now, schedule important but not urgent work, delegate urgent but less important tasks, and drop the rest. The Pareto principle suggests that a small share of causes often produces most results, so focus effort there.

## Manage relationships and stress

Office politics are the informal ways people gain influence. Ethical political skill means building genuine relationships, giving credit, keeping commitments, and staying out of gossip. Unethical moves include hiding information or taking credit for others' work.

In a relationship crisis, such as a public argument or a loss of trust between team members, act quickly and privately, hear each side, focus on behavior and its effects, agree on specific next steps, and follow up. Stress can be eustress, which motivates, or distress, which harms health and performance. Leaders reduce distress by clarifying priorities, balancing workloads, allowing control over how work is done, and modeling breaks. Personal strategies include planning, exercise, sleep, and asking for help early.

## Key terms

**Transformational leadership.** Leadership that inspires followers to pursue a shared vision beyond self-interest.

**Referent power.** Influence based on others' respect and admiration for the leader.

**Organizational culture.** The shared values, beliefs, and practices that shape behavior in an organization.

**Groupthink.** Poor group decisions caused by pressure to agree.

**Eustress.** Positive stress that motivates performance.
