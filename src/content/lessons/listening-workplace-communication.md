## Listen for meaning

Active listening means paying attention, withholding premature judgment, asking useful questions, and checking understanding. A paraphrase restates the speaker’s meaning in your own words; it is not agreement. Reading to understand similarly requires separating central ideas, details, inference, and unsupported claims. Minimize distractions and notice nonverbal signals without assuming one gesture proves a person’s intent.

Use open questions to explore and closed questions to confirm specific details. Distinguish literal information from an inference that needs evidence. A summary captures several key points; a paraphrase checks a particular meaning. In a reading passage, identify who made a claim and whether the passage actually supports it rather than importing an assumption from your own experience.

## Speak clearly and check understanding

Organize speaking around a clear purpose, a few supported points, and a next action. Use a pace and volume suited to the room, explain unfamiliar terms, and pause where listeners need time. Visual aids should support the explanation rather than duplicate a script. For questions, listen fully, clarify if needed, answer directly, and acknowledge limits. Inventing a confident answer can damage trust.

> **Example**
>
> A customer says, “The order was wrong and nobody answers.”
>
> Respond: “You received the wrong items and have had trouble reaching us. Which order number should I check?” This confirms the concern and obtains useful information.

## Maintain professional relationships

Adapt language and channel to context without stereotyping. In disagreement, separate observable facts from interpretation and express the effect and requested change. Respect personal space, confidentiality, and cultural differences. Written digital messages lack vocal cues, so reread tone and avoid unnecessary reply-all messages. A meeting should end with decisions, owners, and deadlines. Communication is successful when shared understanding supports action, not merely when the sender has spoken.

## Key terms

**Paraphrase.** Restating meaning in your own words to check understanding.

**Feedback.** Information showing how a message was received or how work can improve.
