## Measure output and purchasing power

GDP measures final goods and services produced within a country during a period. Counting flour and the bread containing it double counts intermediate production. Nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP adjusts for price change. Inflation is a broad rise in the price level, not just one product becoming expensive. Deflation is a declining price level.

Consumer spending is an important demand indicator, but increases can reflect prices as well as real purchases. Interest-rate changes affect borrowing costs, saving incentives, asset values, and exchange rates. Different sectors can respond at different speeds.

> **Example**
>
> Nominal output rises 8% while the price level rises 5%.
>
> Real growth is approximately 3%; exactly, 1.08 / 1.05 − 1 is about 2.86%.

## Understand employment

The labor force comprises employed people and unemployed people actively seeking work under the measure’s definitions. Unemployment rate = unemployed / labor force. Frictional unemployment accompanies job search; structural unemployment involves skills or location mismatch; cyclical unemployment follows weak overall demand. Expansion, peak, contraction, and trough describe the business cycle. Interpret several indicators over time rather than assuming one month establishes a trend.

## Distinguish fiscal and monetary policy

Fiscal policy changes government taxation and spending. Monetary policy influences money and credit conditions through the central bank. Expansionary policy supports demand; contractionary policy restrains it. Lower interest rates may encourage borrowing, but expectations and credit conditions affect the response. Automatic stabilizers operate without new legislation each downturn. Policies have trade-offs: supporting demand can also raise price pressure when productive capacity is constrained. A policy move does not guarantee a particular outcome.

Taxes can fund public services, redistribute resources, and influence behavior. A progressive tax takes a rising share as its base increases; proportional taxes apply a constant rate; regressive effects place a larger relative burden on lower-income households. Evaluate the tax base and incidence rather than judging only the posted percentage.

## Key terms

**Real GDP.** Output adjusted for price changes.

**Fiscal policy.** Government taxation and spending decisions.
