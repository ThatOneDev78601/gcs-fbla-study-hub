## Start with purpose and constraints

Define the event purpose, audience, attendance target, service level, budget, location, and success measures. Separate planning, which sets objectives and arrangements, from management, which coordinates delivery and changes. List deliverables and dependencies: venue confirmation affects layout, capacity, accessibility, equipment, and promotion. Assign an owner and deadline to each task. Event types such as workshops, trade shows, performances, and fundraisers need different service designs and metrics.

## Build a realistic operating plan

An event budget includes fixed and variable expenses, expected revenue, donations, in-kind contributions, and a contingency allowance. Keep cash costs distinct from donated value. Plan staffing by function, workload, training, shift, and backup coverage; volunteers still need clear responsibilities. A run-of-show records time, action, location, owner, and equipment. Check registration, transport, queues, signage, food needs, accessible routes, sanitation, communications, and teardown.

> **Example**
>
> A workshop has $400 venue cost, $200 equipment cost, and $6 materials per attendee. Forty attendees pay $25 each.
>
> Revenue is $1,000; expense is $600 + $240 = $840, leaving $160 before other costs. If attendance falls to 30, revenue is $750 and expense is $780, creating a $30 shortfall. Test attendance assumptions before committing.

## Prepare for change and evaluate

Risk assessment considers likelihood and impact, then chooses prevention, mitigation, transfer, or acceptance. Insurance may transfer specified financial risks but does not eliminate safety duties. A contingency plan names triggers, decision makers, actions, and communication for weather, power failure, absent staff, or low turnout. Afterward, compare attendance, satisfaction, actual budget, operational incidents, and objectives. Record what should change next time and distinguish plans from outcomes.

- Common mistake: listing an emergency without specifying who acts and when.
- Common mistake: counting an in-kind donation as spendable cash.
- Common mistake: claiming success from attendance while ignoring the event’s actual purpose.

## Key terms

**Run-of-show.** A timed operating schedule specifying actions and responsibilities.

**Contingency plan.** Predefined responses to significant disruptions.

**In-kind contribution.** A donated good or service rather than cash.
