## Understand the connected experience

Hospitality and tourism include lodging, food and beverage, transportation, attractions, travel services, and events. These segments depend on one another: a transport delay can affect hotel arrivals and event attendance. Service is often perishable because an unused room-night cannot be sold tomorrow as yesterday’s room. Demand can be seasonal and sensitive to income, business conditions, weather, and perceived safety. Careers range from front-office and food-service operations to revenue management, destination marketing, and event coordination.

## Design a dependable guest journey

Map discovery, booking, arrival, participation, departure, and follow-up. Confirm reservations, preferences, access needs, cancellation terms, and guarantees accurately. Distribution may use direct bookings, agents, or online platforms, each with different costs and control. Guests choose based on location, price, trust, experience, amenities, and accessibility. Mobile check-in and messaging can improve convenience but need a workable alternative for guests without a suitable device.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A conference hotel expects arrivals to double during a two-hour window. Its booking platform shows different cancellation terms from the hotel confirmation.
>
> Reconcile the published terms, prepare peak staffing and queue communication, confirm access requests, and give guests a consistent arrival message. Technology should support a clear process rather than duplicate conflicting information.

## Control information, money, and risk

Use reliable booking and demand data to plan staffing, inventory, and capacity. Separate cash custody from reconciliation where possible, record transactions, verify authorized refunds, and protect payment data. Security includes identity verification, controlled access, emergency procedures, and responsible handling of guest information. Sustainability involves energy, water, waste, procurement, and transport choices; assess measurable tradeoffs instead of making unsupported green claims. Review guarantees and promotions to ensure the operation can deliver them.

- Common mistake: assuming occupancy alone proves profitability; prices, channel commissions, and service costs matter.
- Common mistake: treating all guests as comfortable with an app-only process.
- Common mistake: collecting sensitive travel details without a service need.

## Key terms to remember

**Perishable service.** Capacity that cannot be stored for sale at a later date.

**Guest journey.** The sequence of interactions before, during, and after a visit.

**Distribution channel.** A route through which guests discover and book a service.
