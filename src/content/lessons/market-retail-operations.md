## Receive and store accurately

Receiving compares delivered goods with the purchase order and packing slip. Count items, inspect damage, record discrepancies, and follow the procedure for accepting or rejecting stock. Label and route merchandise to the correct storage or sales-floor location; update records when it moves. Safe storage considers weight, temperature, security, access, and perishability. FIFO moves older stock first; expiry-sensitive stock may require first-expiry-first-out instead. Never block aisles or emergency access.

## Track what actually exists

A perpetual system updates inventory as transactions occur; a periodic system uses counts at intervals. Physical counts verify the records because theft, breakage, recording mistakes, and returns can create discrepancies. Shrinkage is the difference between recorded and actual inventory, measured on a consistent cost or retail basis. Reorder planning considers sales rate, lead time, and safety stock. Report an out-of-stock promptly, check other locations, and record lost-demand information.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A store expects 120 mugs at $5 cost each but counts 114. Average demand is 10 per day and replenishment takes four days.
>
> The missing six mugs represent $30 of shrinkage at cost. Investigate transaction and receiving errors before assuming theft. A basic reorder point is 40 mugs plus the chosen safety stock.

## Control transactions and movement

Prepare a cash drawer with an authorized starting float. Record each sale, tender, refund, and exchange under policy, then reconcile expected cash with the count. Separate authorization, handling, and reconciliation where possible. Never store unnecessary card details or share login credentials. Shipment choices balance cost, speed, reliability, tracking, handling needs, and environmental impact. Allocation decisions use location-level demand rather than dividing every shipment equally.

- Common mistake: recording a return without checking whether it can safely return to available stock.
- Common mistake: confronting a suspected shoplifter instead of following trained safety procedures.
- Common mistake: treating book inventory as unquestionable after a mismatch.

## Key terms to remember

**Shrinkage.** Recorded inventory minus inventory actually present, on a consistent valuation basis.

**Lead time.** The time between placing an order and receiving usable stock.

**Perpetual inventory.** Inventory records continuously updated by transactions.
