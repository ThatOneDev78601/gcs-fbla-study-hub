## Discover before recommending

Selling helps a buyer select an appropriate solution, rather than pressuring everyone into the same purchase. Establish rapport, ask open questions about needs and constraints, listen, then confirm understanding. Research accurate product specifications and company selling policies before making claims. A customer choosing shoes for occasional walking has different needs from a competitive runner; recommending the most expensive model without understanding that distinction can damage trust.

## Demonstrate value and respond to objections

Translate features into benefits relevant to the buyer. A demonstration should show how the product works, invite appropriate interaction, and address limitations honestly. Objections reveal uncertainty about fit, value, timing, risk, or authority. Clarify the concern, acknowledge it, provide evidence, and check whether it is resolved. Do not invent capabilities, misrepresent scarcity, or guarantee results outside your control. Digital tools can show specifications and availability, but information still needs verification.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A shopper says a $40 reusable bottle costs too much and needs one that fits a narrow bag pocket.
>
> Ask about budget and pocket size. Demonstrate fit and durability, compare a suitable lower-priced option, and explain the actual warranty. The right result may be a cheaper product or no sale.

## Close and support the relationship

A close asks for a decision after the needs and concerns have been addressed. Summarize the agreed solution, explain total price and terms, and offer a clear next step. Suggestion selling recommends a genuinely useful complement rather than an unrelated extra. Process the transaction and documentation accurately, confirm delivery or pickup, and explain support and returns. Follow up when appropriate and with permission. Service quality after the sale supports repeat business and the brand promise.

- Common mistake: answering an objection before finding out what the buyer actually means.
- Common mistake: treating a hesitant customer as a challenge to defeat.
- Practice: explain one product feature, its customer-specific benefit, and a limitation.

## Key terms to remember

**Needs assessment.** Questions and listening used to identify the buyer’s situation and priorities.

**Objection.** A concern or uncertainty that may prevent a buying decision.

**Suggestion selling.** Recommending a relevant complementary product or service.
