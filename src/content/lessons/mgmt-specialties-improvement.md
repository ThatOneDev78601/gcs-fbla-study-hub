## Management specialties compared

Six Sigma aims for no more than 3.4 defects per million opportunities. Lean targets waste such as waiting, excess inventory, unnecessary movement, overproduction, and defects. Both use data, but Lean emphasizes speed and flow while Six Sigma emphasizes reducing variation.

| Specialty | Focus | Key tools or ideas |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project management | A temporary effort with a defined goal, budget, and deadline | Scope, schedule, work breakdown structure, critical path |
| Information management | Collecting, storing, and protecting data for decisions | Databases, dashboards, data governance |
| Customer relationship management (CRM) | Building lasting customer relationships | Customer records, segmentation, service history |
| Supply chain management | Moving materials and products from suppliers to customers | Forecasting, inventory, logistics |
| Change management | Helping people adopt new processes | Lewin's unfreeze, change, refreeze; Kotter's eight steps beginning with a sense of urgency |
| Knowledge management | Capturing and sharing what employees know | Explicit knowledge (documented) versus tacit knowledge (experience); wikis, mentoring |
| Quality management | Meeting customer requirements consistently | Total quality management; Six Sigma's DMAIC (define, measure, analyze, improve, control); Lean waste reduction; Deming's plan-do-check-act; ISO 9001 |
| Risk management | Identifying and treating threats to goals | Risk register, likelihood and impact ratings |

## Map a process and find the bottleneck

A workflow diagram shows each step, who does it, and how work moves. A process can go no faster than its slowest step, the bottleneck. Improving any other step does not raise output.

> **Example**
>
> A print shop's order process has four steps with these hourly capacities: design review 40 orders, printing 25 orders, cutting 35 orders, and packing 50 orders. The shop receives 30 orders an hour at peak.
>
> 1. Throughput equals the smallest capacity: 25 orders an hour, set by printing.
> 2. At 30 orders arriving and 25 printed, a queue grows by 5 orders an hour, so customers wait longer through the peak.
> 3. Adding a second printer that brings printing to 45 orders an hour moves the bottleneck to cutting at 35, which now covers the 30-order demand.
>
> Invest in printing first. A faster packing station would not raise output at all. After the fix, monitor cutting, the new limiting step, and check whether the printer's cost is covered by the extra orders.

## Build and read a department budget

An operating budget plans revenue and expenses for a period. A capital budget plans large purchases of long-term assets. Budget variances compare actual results with the budget. A flexible budget adjusts expected costs to the actual activity level, which gives a fairer comparison than the original static budget.

Department budgets connect to the statements. Operating expenses appear on the income statement. A new machine appears on the balance sheet as an asset and in investing activities on the cash flow statement, and only its depreciation hits the income statement.

| Customer support department | Budget | Actual | Variance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wages | $48,000 | $51,600 | $3,600 U |
| Software subscriptions | $6,000 | $6,000 | $0 |
| Training | $3,000 | $1,200 | $1,800 F |
| Supplies | $1,500 | $1,700 | $200 U |
| Total | $58,500 | $60,500 | $2,000 U |

- Investigate large variances, not every small one. Here wages are over by 7.5%. Check whether overtime rose because ticket volume grew.
- The training savings may be a warning sign: cutting training can raise errors and future costs.

## Work a full management case

Management cases ask you to diagnose a problem and defend a plan. Use the functions of management as a checklist: planning (goals and strategy), organizing (structure and resources), staffing (hiring and training), directing or leading (motivation and communication), and controlling (measuring and correcting). Controls can be feedforward (preventing problems before work starts), concurrent (checking during work), or feedback (reviewing results). A balanced scorecard measures success in four areas: financial, customer, internal process, and learning and growth.

> **Try it yourself**
>
> A regional bakery chain's newest store has 20% lower sales than the others, more customer complaints about wait times, and high staff turnover. Using the management functions, diagnose the problem and propose a plan with two measures of success.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

A strong answer gathers evidence first: compare staffing levels, training records, and wait times by hour with other stores. Planning: set a goal such as cutting peak wait time to under 4 minutes within 90 days. Organizing: add a second register during peak hours and move prep work to slower periods. Staffing: use the chain's training program and assign a mentor from a strong store. Leading: hold short daily check-ins and recognize improvements. Controlling: track average wait time and staff turnover weekly, with sales per labor hour as a financial check. It should mention ethics, such as fair scheduling, and the cost of the changes.

</details>

## Key terms

**Bottleneck.** The slowest step in a process, which limits total output.

**Six Sigma.** A quality method that uses data to reduce variation and defects.

**Tacit knowledge.** Know-how gained from experience that is hard to write down.

**Flexible budget.** A budget adjusted to the actual level of activity.

**Balanced scorecard.** A performance system measuring financial, customer, process, and learning goals.
