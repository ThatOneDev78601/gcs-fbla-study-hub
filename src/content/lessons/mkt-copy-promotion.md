## Headlines and body copy

The headline's job is to make the right reader stop. Strong headlines promise a benefit, name the audience, raise a question, or share news, and they are specific: "Fix a flat in 10 minutes, no tools needed" beats "Great bike products." Write ten headlines before choosing one.

Body copy supports the headline. Lead with the benefit, back it with proof such as a feature, number, or testimonial, address the likely objection, and end with a clear call to action (CTA) that says exactly what to do next. Use "you" and short sentences. Features describe the product; benefits describe what the customer gains. A 2,000 mAh battery is a feature; "charge your phone twice on one battery" is the benefit.

> **Example**
>
> Write a print ad for a fictional reusable lunch container aimed at busy parents.
>
> 1. Headline: "Pack lunch in 2 minutes. Wash it in 30 seconds."
> 2. Body: "SnapBox's three sealed sections keep sandwiches dry and fruit fresh. It goes straight into the dishwasher, and its leak-proof lid has survived 5,000 test drops."
> 3. Objection: "Worried about price? One SnapBox replaces about 250 plastic bags a year."
> 4. CTA: "Get 15% off your first order at snapbox.example with code LUNCH15 through May 31."
>
> Each line has one job: stop the reader, prove the benefit, answer the doubt, and drive one action with a deadline. Every claim, such as the drop test, must be true and documented.

## Email, social posts, and storytelling

A marketing email needs a subject line that is specific and honest, preview text that adds to it, one main message, a visible CTA button, and an unsubscribe link. Social posts must work in the first line or the first three seconds of video, before a viewer scrolls. Match the platform's style: short vertical video, a striking image with a short caption, or a longer professional post.

Brand storytelling follows a simple arc: a character (often the customer) with a problem, a struggle, the brand as a helper, and a better outcome. Stories should be true or clearly presented as illustrations.

Adapting content means keeping the core message and changing format. A 30-second video becomes a 6-second cut for pre-roll ads, a still image with a caption, an email feature, and a poster with the headline and QR code.

## Sales promotion tools

A tagline is a short, lasting phrase that sums up the brand promise, used across campaigns. A slogan may belong to one campaign. Good taglines are short, distinctive, true, and easy to say. Store signage follows a hierarchy: exterior identification, directional signs, department signs, and point-of-purchase signs with product and price details.

| Tool | Purpose | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coupon or discount code | Encourage trial or a quick purchase | Training customers to wait for deals |
| Rebate | Lower net price after purchase | Low redemption can feel deceptive if terms are hidden |
| Free sample | Let people experience the product | Cost per sample |
| Premium (gift with purchase) | Add value without cutting price | Gift quality reflects on the brand |
| Contest | Engagement based on skill | Clear rules and judging criteria |
| Sweepstakes | Engagement based on chance | Must not require a purchase to enter; otherwise it can be an illegal lottery |
| Loyalty program | Reward repeat purchases | Rewards must feel attainable |
| Point-of-purchase display and signage | Prompt decisions in the store | Clutter and outdated prices |
| Special event | Create a reason to visit | Staffing and weather |
| Cross-promotion | Two brands promote each other to share audiences | Brand fit and clear responsibilities |

> **Try it yourself**
>
> A fictional neighborhood bookstore and a coffee shop next door want a joint promotion for a slow February. Design a cross-promotion: the offer, how each business promotes it, how it is tracked, and one rule to prevent problems.

## Key terms

**Call to action.** A direct instruction telling the audience what to do next.

**Benefit.** What the customer gains from a product feature.

**Sweepstakes.** A promotion with prizes awarded by chance, which must not require a purchase.

**Cross-promotion.** Two or more brands promoting each other to reach shared audiences.

**Tagline.** A short, lasting phrase that expresses a brand's promise.
