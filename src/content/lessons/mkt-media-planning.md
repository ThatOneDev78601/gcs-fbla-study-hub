## The planning vocabulary

A media plan decides where, when, and how often an advertisement runs. Reach is the percentage (or number) of the target audience exposed to the message at least once during a period. Frequency is the average number of times each reached person is exposed. Gross rating points (GRPs) equal reach multiplied by frequency. Impressions are the total number of exposures, including repeats.

Cost per thousand (CPM) is the cost of 1,000 impressions: cost divided by impressions, times 1,000. It lets you compare channels of different sizes. Cost per point (CPP) is the cost of one rating point. Digital channels also report cost per click (CPC) and cost per acquisition (CPA).

| Measure | Formula | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GRPs | Reach (%) x frequency | 50% reach x 4 = 200 GRPs |
| Impressions | Audience size x GRPs / 100 | 100,000 people x 200 / 100 = 200,000 |
| CPM | Cost / impressions x 1,000 | $1,500 / 300,000 x 1,000 = $5.00 |
| Cost per point | Cost / GRPs | $6,000 / 200 = $30 per point |
| CPC | Cost / clicks | $600 / 400 = $1.50 |

## Compare channels

A lower CPM is not automatically better. Compare how well the audience matches the target, how the message works in that medium, the lead time, and whether results can be measured.

Television builds broad reach with sight and sound, at high cost. Radio offers local targeting and frequency at lower cost, but no visuals. Print magazines target interests and last longer; newspapers reach local, often older readers. Outdoor ads such as billboards build frequency along routes but allow only a few words. Digital display, search, social, and video ads can target narrowly and report results quickly, though ad blockers and fraud reduce real impressions. Direct mail is personal and measurable but costly per piece.

> **Example**
>
> A fictional climbing gym wants to reach adults 18 to 34 within 15 miles. Option A: local radio, $800 for spots reaching an estimated 120,000 listener impressions, about 30% in the target age. Option B: social video ads, $900 for 180,000 impressions, targeted by age and location so about 85% are in the target.
>
> 1. Radio CPM: 800 / 120,000 x 1,000 = $6.67. Target impressions: 120,000 x 30% = 36,000. Target CPM: 800 / 36,000 x 1,000 = $22.22.
> 2. Social CPM: 900 / 180,000 x 1,000 = $5.00. Target impressions: 180,000 x 85% = 153,000. Target CPM: 900 / 153,000 x 1,000 = $5.88.
>
> Social video reaches the target at about a quarter of the cost per target impression. Radio could still help if the gym wants broader awareness, but the social option fits this goal and can be measured by clicks and sign-ups.

## Build a schedule and a coordinated plan

Scheduling patterns include continuity (steady advertising all year), flighting (bursts with gaps of no advertising), and pulsing (a steady base with bursts at peak times). A ski shop might pulse: light all year with heavy bursts in November and December.

Integrated marketing communications (IMC) coordinates advertising, public relations, sales promotion, personal selling, and digital channels so they share one message and look. Each channel has a role: one builds awareness, another drives action, another rewards loyalty. A plan states the objective, target audience, key message, channels and their roles, schedule, budget, and how results will be measured.

| Week | Channel | Role | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1-2 | Social video (geo-targeted) | Awareness of grand reopening | $900 |
| 1-4 | Search ads on "climbing gym near me" | Capture people already looking | $600 |
| 2 | Local newspaper feature (press release) | Credibility through publicity | $0 |
| 3 | Free first-visit coupon on social and in-store | Trial (sales promotion) | $400 in redeemed value |
| 4 | Email to new sign-ups | Convert trial visitors to members | $50 |

> **Try it yourself**
>
> A target audience has 250,000 people. A plan aims for 40% reach with an average frequency of 5, at a total cost of $12,500. Calculate GRPs, impressions, CPM, and cost per point.

## Key terms

**Reach.** The share of the target audience exposed to an ad at least once in a period.

**Frequency.** The average number of times a reached person sees an ad.

**Gross rating points.** Reach multiplied by frequency; a measure of total advertising weight.

**CPM.** Cost per thousand impressions.

**Pulsing.** A schedule with steady advertising plus heavier bursts at peak times.

**Integrated marketing communications.** Coordinating all promotional tools to deliver one consistent message.
