## Follow the six-step main-motion path

A member obtains recognition and makes a motion; another member seconds it when required; the chair states the question; members debate; the chair puts the question to a vote; the chair announces the result. A second means another member wants consideration, not necessarily agreement. Once stated by the chair, the motion belongs to the assembly. Amendments change wording and must be germane; vote on an amendment before the affected main motion.

> **Example**
>
> A member moves to spend $100; an amendment changes $100 to $80.
>
> Resolve the amendment first. If adopted, then debate and vote on the main motion to spend $80. Adopting the amendment alone does not adopt the spending proposal.

## Choose the right class

Main motions introduce business. Subsidiary motions help dispose of a pending question, such as amend, commit, or postpone. Privileged motions address urgent assembly needs. Incidental motions handle procedural questions arising from business, such as a point of order or appeal. Motions bringing a question again before the assembly include reconsider and rescind. Laying on the table temporarily sets aside business for an urgent matter; it should not be confused with postponing to a chosen time or defeating a proposal.

## Manage precedence and debate

Higher-ranking motions can take precedence over lower-ranking pending motions in the relevant circumstances. Study both rank and the rule for debate, amendment, and voting; not every motion is debatable or requires the same threshold. Address remarks to the chair and discuss the question without personal attacks. A demand for an immediate vote is not automatically adopted: the previous question requires the appropriate vote. Consult the adopted authority for exceptions, qualifications, and special circumstances.

In an ordinary deliberative assembly under default rules, a member ordinarily has up to two speeches on the same question in one day, up to ten minutes each, and must yield priority to someone who has not spoken. The assembly’s rules can change these limits. A primary amendment can itself be amended once; an amendment to an amendment is resolved before the primary amendment. Reconsider generally concerns a recent vote and has special maker and timing requirements. Rescind addresses an earlier action, subject to notice, vote, and limits on what can be undone.

| Rank: low to high | Motion | Usual function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Main motion | Introduce business |
| 2 | Postpone indefinitely | Dispose without a direct vote on the main question |
| 3 | Amend | Change germane wording |
| 4 | Commit or refer | Send for committee work |
| 5 | Postpone definitely | Defer to a specified time |
| 6 | Limit or extend debate | Change debate limits; normally two thirds |
| 7 | Previous question | Close debate; normally two thirds |
| 8 | Lay on the table | Temporarily set aside for urgent intervening business |
| 9 | Call for orders of the day | Require the adopted order, subject to applicable rules |
| 10 | Raise a question of privilege | Address urgent rights or comfort |
| 11 | Recess | Take a short intermission |
| 12 | Adjourn | End the meeting |
| 13 | Fix the time to which to adjourn | Set an adjourned meeting |

## Key terms

**Pending.** Before the assembly and not yet disposed of.

**Germane.** Relevant to the subject of the motion being amended.
