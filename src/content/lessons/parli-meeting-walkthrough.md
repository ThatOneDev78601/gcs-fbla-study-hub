## Terms you must use precisely

Members' basic rights include attending meetings, making motions, debating, voting, and nominating and being nominated. Rules protect the rights of the majority to decide, the minority to be heard, individual members, and absent members. That is why some actions, such as rescinding without notice, require more than a majority.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Meeting | A single gathering of members to transact business without leaving the room except for a short recess |
| Session | A meeting or series of connected meetings, such as a multi-day convention |
| Quorum | The minimum number of members needed to transact business; if the bylaws are silent, a majority of the membership |
| Pending | A motion that has been stated by the chair and not yet disposed of |
| Immediately pending | The motion most recently stated, which is voted on first |
| General (unanimous) consent | Adopting a routine action without a formal vote when no one objects |
| Pro tem | Temporarily, such as a chair pro tem presiding in the president's absence |
| Ex officio | A member of a body because of another office held; has full rights unless the rules say otherwise |
| Obtain the floor | Being recognized by the chair, which gives the right to speak |

## The standard order of business

The presiding officer refers to themself as "the chair," remains impartial, states each question, recognizes speakers, and announces results. The secretary records minutes, keeps official documents, and reads the minutes for approval.

1. Call to order: the chair confirms a quorum and says, "The meeting will come to order."
2. Reading and approval of minutes: "Are there any corrections to the minutes? Hearing none, the minutes stand approved as read." Corrections are usually made by general consent.
3. Reports of officers, boards, and standing committees. A treasurer's report is filed, not adopted; an auditor's report is adopted.
4. Reports of special committees.
5. Special orders: business given priority for this meeting.
6. Unfinished business and general orders: items carried over, such as motions postponed to this meeting. Never call it "old business."
7. New business.
8. Adjournment, often by general consent when business is complete.

## Handling a main motion

A main motion brings business before the assembly. It takes six steps: three that bring it before the assembly and three that consider it. Before step one, the member must obtain the floor.

1. A member makes the motion: "I move that the chapter hold a car wash on May 9."
2. Another member seconds it: "Second." A second shows that at least two members want it discussed, not that the seconder agrees.
3. The chair states the question: "It is moved and seconded that the chapter hold a car wash on May 9. Is there any debate?" The motion now belongs to the assembly.
4. Members debate. The maker has the first right to speak.
5. The chair puts the question: "Those in favor, say aye. Those opposed, say no."
6. The chair announces the result: "The ayes have it and the motion is adopted. The chapter will hold a car wash on May 9."

- Each member may speak twice on the same question on the same day, 10 minutes each time, unless the rules say otherwise. A member who has not spoken gets preference over one who has.
- Remarks are addressed to the chair, must be germane to the pending question, and must not attack motives or persons.
- The maker may vote against their own motion but may not speak against it.

## What the minutes contain

Minutes are a record of what was done, not what was said. They include the kind of meeting (regular or special), the organization's name, date, time, and place, whether the regular chair and secretary were present, whether the previous minutes were approved, every main motion (except withdrawn ones) with its exact wording and how it was disposed of, points of order and appeals with their results, and the time of adjournment. They do not include personal opinions or summaries of debate. The secretary signs them; some organizations also have the president sign.

> **Example**
>
> Write the minutes entry for a motion that was amended and adopted.
>
> "Jamie Ortiz moved that the chapter hold a car wash on May 9. The motion was amended to add "from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. at the school parking lot." The motion as amended was adopted." The entry records the maker, the final wording, and the result. Many organizations omit the seconder's name.

## Key terms

**Quorum.** The minimum number of members required to conduct business.

**Immediately pending question.** The most recently stated motion, which must be resolved first.

**General consent.** Approval of a routine matter when no member objects.

**Ex officio.** Holding membership on a body because of another office.

**Unfinished business.** Business carried over from a previous meeting; the correct term instead of "old business."
