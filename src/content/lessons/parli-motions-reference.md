## The 13 ranking motions

The main motion is the lowest-ranking motion. Seven subsidiary motions help dispose of a main motion, and five privileged motions deal with urgent matters unrelated to the pending business. A higher-ranking motion can be made while a lower one is pending, and it is voted on first. The table runs from highest rank (1) to lowest (13). These are the standard rules under Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised; the full rules include exceptions and conditions.

| Rank | Motion | Class | Second? | Debatable? | Amendable? | Vote |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fix the time to which to adjourn | Privileged | Yes | No | Yes | Majority |
| 2 | Adjourn | Privileged | Yes | No | No | Majority |
| 3 | Recess | Privileged | Yes | No | Yes | Majority |
| 4 | Raise a question of privilege | Privileged | No | No | No | Chair rules |
| 5 | Call for the orders of the day | Privileged | No | No | No | Chair enforces on one member's demand; two-thirds to set aside |
| 6 | Lay on the table | Subsidiary | Yes | No | No | Majority |
| 7 | Previous question (close debate) | Subsidiary | Yes | No | No | Two-thirds |
| 8 | Limit or extend limits of debate | Subsidiary | Yes | No | Yes | Two-thirds |
| 9 | Postpone to a certain time (postpone definitely) | Subsidiary | Yes | Yes, on the postponement only | Yes | Majority |
| 10 | Commit or refer | Subsidiary | Yes | Yes, on the referral only | Yes | Majority |
| 11 | Amend | Subsidiary | Yes | If the motion being amended is debatable | Yes (a primary amendment can be amended once) | Majority |
| 12 | Postpone indefinitely | Subsidiary | Yes | Yes, and opens the main question to debate | No | Majority |
| 13 | Main motion | Main | Yes | Yes | Yes | Majority |

- A memory aid: motions that limit members' rights (previous question, limit debate, suspend the rules) need two-thirds.
- Lay on the table is only for setting business aside temporarily for something urgent. Using it to kill a motion is improper; postpone indefinitely is the motion for avoiding a vote.

## Incidental motions and motions that bring a question back

When several motions are pending, vote on the immediately pending one first, then work down. For example, if a main motion, an amendment, and a motion to refer are pending, vote first on referring. If referral fails, vote on the amendment, then on the main motion as amended or not.

| Motion | Purpose | Key rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Point of order | Call attention to a rule violation | No second; not debatable; chair rules; made promptly when the error occurs |
| Appeal | Challenge the chair's ruling | Second; usually debatable; a majority or tie sustains the chair |
| Suspend the rules | Do something the rules otherwise prevent | Second; not debatable; usually two-thirds; cannot suspend bylaws |
| Objection to consideration | Prevent an unwanted main motion from being discussed | No second; not debatable; made before debate; two-thirds against consideration sustains it |
| Division of a question | Split a motion with separate parts | Second; not debatable; majority |
| Division of the assembly | Demand a rising vote when a voice vote seems unclear | No second; one member can demand it |
| Parliamentary inquiry / request for information | Ask about procedure or facts | No second; chair or speaker answers |
| Take from the table | Resume business laid on the table | Second; not debatable; majority |
| Reconsider | Vote again on a question decided at the same session | Made only by a member who voted on the prevailing side; debatable if the original motion was; majority |
| Rescind or amend something previously adopted | Cancel or change an earlier decision | Second; debatable; two-thirds, or majority with previous notice, or majority of the entire membership |
| Discharge a committee | Take a referred matter back from a committee | Same vote options as rescind |

## Calculate the vote

A majority vote means more than half of the votes cast, not counting blanks or abstentions. Two-thirds means at least two-thirds of the votes cast. Some rules use a different base, such as a majority of the members present or a majority of the entire membership, so always check the base. A tie fails a majority vote. A plurality, the most votes, elects only when the bylaws allow it.

The chair of a large assembly votes when the vote is by ballot or when their vote would change the result, such as breaking or creating a tie, or reaching or blocking two-thirds. In a small board, the chair may vote like other members. Abstaining is a choice not to vote, and it is not counted as a vote.

> **Example**
>
> A chapter has 60 members. Forty attend. On a motion, 22 vote yes, 14 vote no, and 4 abstain.
>
> 1. Votes cast: 22 + 14 = 36.
> 2. Majority of votes cast: more than 18, so 19 needed. The motion passes with 22.
> 3. Two-thirds of votes cast: 36 x 2/3 = 24 needed. A two-thirds motion would fail with 22.
> 4. Majority of the entire membership: more than 30, so 31 needed. That would fail.
>
> The same vote passes or fails depending on the rule. Name the base before you calculate.

## Nominations, elections, committees, and boards

Nominations can come from the chair, from the floor (no second needed), from a nominating committee, or by ballot. A nominating committee's report does not prevent floor nominations. Closing nominations requires a two-thirds vote, and reopening them requires a majority. Elections are often by ballot, especially when the bylaws require it, and a candidate needs a majority unless the bylaws allow a plurality. An election takes effect immediately if the candidate is present and does not decline, unless the bylaws set another start date.

Standing committees handle ongoing work and are usually named in the bylaws. Special (ad hoc) committees handle one task and dissolve when they report. A committee of the whole is the entire assembly acting as a committee, used for informal discussion. Committees report and recommend; the assembly decides. A board acts only within the authority the bylaws give it and may meet in executive session when confidentiality is needed. The vice president presides when the president is absent or must leave the chair; the treasurer receives, pays, and reports on funds.

>

## Key terms

**Privileged motion.** A high-ranking motion about urgent matters not related to pending business, such as adjourning or recessing.

**Subsidiary motion.** A motion that helps dispose of a main motion, such as amend or refer.

**Previous question.** A motion to end debate and vote immediately; requires two-thirds.

**Reconsider.** A motion, made by a member on the prevailing side, to vote again on a question decided at the same session.

**Plurality.** The largest number of votes, which may be less than a majority.
