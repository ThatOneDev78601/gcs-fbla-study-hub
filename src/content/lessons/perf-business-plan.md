## Validate a real customer problem

A business plan explains who the customer is, what problem matters, how the offer solves it, and why the venture can deliver sustainably. Research the industry, market size, trends, direct and indirect competitors, and customer behavior. Avoid treating personal enthusiasm as evidence of demand. Compare alternatives already available to the customer, including doing nothing. State a differentiated value proposition and test the riskiest assumptions with relevant primary and secondary research.

## Connect marketing and operations

The marketing plan identifies the target, positioning, product, pricing, channels, promotion, and sales process. Operations describes location, production or service delivery, suppliers, capacity, quality, inventory, staffing, and responsibilities. The organization section explains roles, capabilities, and resource needs. Legal structure and compliance requirements should be checked using current official guidance. Make the sections agree: a same-day service promise needs enough capacity, supplies, scheduling, and trained people to deliver it.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A fictional mobile bicycle-repair venture forecasts 20 jobs per day, but its technician can complete only six and travel takes additional time.
>
> Revise capacity, staffing, service area, scheduling, prices, and revenue projections together. The plan cannot use marketing demand as a substitute for the ability to perform the work.

## Model viability and uncertainty

Build revenue from explicit units, prices, timing, and demand assumptions. Separate startup spending, fixed operating costs, variable costs, and working-capital needs. Forecast income and cash flow; a profitable month can still create a cash shortage if customers pay late. Calculate contribution and break-even, then test lower demand, higher costs, or slower collection. Identify risks, contingencies, and milestones. Write the executive summary after the plan is coherent and supported.

- Common mistake: projecting a market share without explaining how customers will be acquired.
- Common mistake: omitting founder labor or necessary operating costs.
- Common mistake: making the financial forecast contradict the staffing and capacity plan.

## Key terms to remember

**Value proposition.** A clear explanation of the benefit offered to a particular customer.

**Working capital.** Resources needed to support day-to-day operations and timing gaps.

**Sensitivity analysis.** Testing how results change when important assumptions change.
