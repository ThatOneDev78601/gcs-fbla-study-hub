## Start with the role

Read the position description or event requirements and identify essential skills, responsibilities, qualifications, and evidence. Research the employer or career field through reliable sources. A résumé is a selective, accurate account of relevant experience rather than a complete autobiography. Use clear headings, consistent dates, precise action verbs, and truthful outcomes. School projects, volunteering, and club responsibilities can demonstrate transferable skills when the connection is explained.

## Build a portfolio with context

Select artifacts that show different relevant capabilities, such as planning, communication, analysis, design, teamwork, or technical execution. For each, explain the task, your role, the process, result, and reflection. Identify team contributions accurately and remove private or confidential information. A polished artifact without context does not show what you personally did. Organize the portfolio for easy retrieval and check the current event’s format and permitted-material rules.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A student helped run a club workshop with four teammates and wants to claim “Managed the entire event.”
>
> State the real responsibility: coordinated registration, checked 45 attendance records, and revised the check-in checklist after feedback. Include the relevant artifact and describe teammates’ roles honestly.

## Answer with specific evidence

For behavioral questions, use situation, task, action, and result to explain an authentic example. Focus on your actions and what you learned rather than reciting a memorized generic strength. For hypothetical questions, clarify assumptions and explain the decision process. Listen to the whole question, speak directly, and acknowledge limits. Prepare relevant questions about the role and follow professional follow-up practices when appropriate. Rehearse concise answers without making them sound identical regardless of the prompt.

- Common mistake: exaggerating responsibility or inventing metrics.
- Common mistake: filling a portfolio with many unrelated artifacts.
- Common mistake: criticizing teammates instead of explaining your own decisions and learning.

## Key terms to remember

**Transferable skill.** A capability useful across different tasks, roles, or settings.

**STAR.** Situation, task, action, and result: a structure for behavioral examples.

**Portfolio artifact.** A work sample used as evidence of a relevant capability.
