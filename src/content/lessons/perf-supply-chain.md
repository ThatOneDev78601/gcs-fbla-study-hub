## Map the flow

A supply chain connects suppliers, production or service preparation, storage, distribution, and customers. Goods, information, and money move through different but related paths. Map locations, responsibilities, capacity, lead times, and handoffs before recommending changes. Procurement selects and manages suppliers; logistics coordinates movement and storage; operations transforms inputs into useful outputs. A change that helps one function may create cost or delay elsewhere, so evaluate the end-to-end result.

## Compare cost and service

Supplier selection considers quality, total cost, reliability, capacity, lead time, ethics, and risk rather than unit price alone. Inventory provides availability and buffers uncertainty but requires cash, storage, and handling. Transportation trades off speed, cost, reliability, size, perishability, and environmental effect. Information systems improve visibility when records and definitions are accurate. Demand variability can be amplified by delayed information and oversized orders, producing unnecessary swings upstream.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A fictional club merchandise supplier offers a cheaper unit price but requires a large minimum order and a six-week lead time. A local supplier charges more but replenishes weekly.
>
> Compare total cost, forecast uncertainty, excess-stock risk, cash use, and required service level. A higher unit price may be worthwhile when demand is uncertain and unsold stock has little value.

## Plan resilience and measure results

Identify critical dependencies, likely disruptions, and the impact of losing a supplier, route, system, or facility. Compare alternatives such as backup suppliers, safety stock, flexible capacity, or revised promises. Define implementation tasks, owners, timeline, resource needs, and monitoring measures. Useful measures include on-time delivery, order accuracy, lead time, stockouts, inventory turnover, and total delivered cost. A recommendation should name the tradeoff it accepts and the evidence that would trigger a revision.

- Common mistake: optimizing freight cost while increasing stockouts or damage.
- Common mistake: claiming zero risk after adding a backup supplier.
- Common mistake: comparing supplier prices without minimum quantities, returns, or freight.

## Key terms to remember

**Total delivered cost.** The combined relevant cost of obtaining and delivering an item.

**Supply-chain resilience.** The ability to prepare for, respond to, and recover from disruption.

**Service level.** A defined measure of fulfillment performance or availability.
