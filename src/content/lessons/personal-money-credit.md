## Connect goals to a budget

Money functions as a medium of exchange, unit of account, and store of value. A useful goal specifies an amount and date. Track income and spending before creating a plan. Gross income is before deductions; take-home income funds the household budget. Fixed expenses are relatively stable, variable expenses change, and irregular expenses still require planning. Review your budget when income, costs, or priorities change.

> **Example**
>
> You need $240 in eight weeks and already saved $40.
>
> The remaining $200 requires $25 per week. Compare that amount with expected take-home income and essential costs.

## Borrow using total cost

Credit lets a borrower use funds now with an obligation to repay. Compare APR, fees, term, total payments, security, and consequences of missed payments. A smaller monthly payment can cost more overall when the term is longer. Simple interest = principal × rate × time, with matching units. Compound interest adds interest to the balance. Credit reports record borrowing history; credit scores summarize information using a model. They are related but not identical.

For annual compounding, future value = present value × (1 + rate)^years. A $100 deposit earning a hypothetical 5% annually becomes $110.25 after two years: $100 × 1.05 × 1.05. Discounting reverses the process to compare a future amount in today’s terms. A nominal return does not remove inflation; a rough real return subtracts inflation, while the exact calculation divides growth factors.

## Protect and adapt the plan

Compare financial providers on fees, access, service, and appropriate account protections. Reconcile statements and report suspicious activity promptly through verified channels. Inflation reduces what a fixed dollar amount buys. Emergency savings, diversification, and insurance address different kinds of risk. Distinguish saving for a near-term need from investing for uncertain future return. Taxes and benefits affect take-home income; use current official information for real filings or product decisions rather than treating a practice example as a recommendation.

Personal net worth equals assets minus liabilities. Income is a flow over time; net worth is a position at a date. An emergency fund addresses unexpected expenses and short-term disruption, while insurance transfers specified large-loss exposures.

## Key terms

**APR.** An annualized borrowing-cost measure defined by applicable rules.

**Compound interest.** Interest calculated on a balance that includes prior interest.
