## Read a pay stub

A pay stub shows gross pay, then pre-tax deductions such as retirement contributions and health premiums, then taxes withheld, then after-tax deductions, and finally net pay. Each line usually shows the current period and the year-to-date (YTD) total. Check the hours, rate, and YTD totals every pay period. Errors are easier to fix right away.

Income comes from wages and salary, tips, self-employment, interest, dividends, capital gains, rent, and government benefits. Earned income comes from work. Unearned income comes from investments and other sources, and some is taxed differently.

## Work a simplified tax return

The real Form 1040 changes every year. This example uses practice numbers chosen for easy math, not the official 2026 brackets or deduction. On a test, use the figures given.

The return follows a fixed order. Total income minus adjustments, such as deductible IRA contributions, gives adjusted gross income (AGI). AGI minus the standard deduction or itemized deductions gives taxable income. The tax comes from the brackets. Credits reduce the tax dollar for dollar. Finally, compare the tax with what was withheld or paid. The difference is a refund or an amount owed.

> **Example**
>
> A single filer earns $30,000 in wages, has no adjustments, takes a practice standard deduction of $16,000, and had $1,700 withheld. Practice brackets: 10% on the first $12,000 of taxable income and 12% above that.
>
> 1. Taxable income: 30,000 - 16,000 = 14,000.
> 2. Tax: 10% x 12,000 = 1,200, plus 12% x 2,000 = 240, for 1,440.
> 3. Refund: 1,700 - 1,440 = 260.
>
> The refund is $260. The marginal rate, which applies to the last dollar, is 12%. The effective rate is 1,440 / 30,000 = 4.8% of total income. Moving into a higher bracket raises the tax only on the income above the bracket line.

## Reconcile a bank account

A bank reconciliation explains why the bank statement and the account register show different balances. Adjust the bank balance for items the bank has not seen yet: add deposits in transit and subtract outstanding checks. Adjust the register for items only the bank knew about: subtract service fees and returned checks, and add interest earned. Both adjusted balances must match.

> **Example**
>
> The statement shows $1,240. A $300 deposit made on the last day is not on it. Checks for $85 and $140 have not cleared. The register shows $1,300. The statement shows a $10 fee and $25 of interest that are not in the register.
>
> 1. Adjusted bank balance: 1,240 + 300 - 85 - 140 = 1,315.
> 2. Adjusted register balance: 1,300 - 10 + 25 = 1,315.
>
> Both equal $1,315, so the account is reconciled. Record the fee and the interest in the register.

## Credit, borrowing, and identity theft

Credit reports come from three national bureaus: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Everyone can get free reports weekly at AnnualCreditReport.com. A FICO score weighs payment history (35%), amounts owed (30%), length of credit history (15%), new credit (10%), and credit mix (10%). Paying on time and keeping card balances low relative to limits matter most.

Revolving credit, such as a credit card, can be reused up to a limit. Installment credit, such as an auto loan, is repaid in fixed payments. Compare loans by APR and total cost. A $2,000 card balance at 24% APR accrues about $40 of interest in one month (2,000 x 0.24 / 12). Paying only the minimum can stretch repayment over years.

If identity theft is suspected, report it at IdentityTheft.gov, place a fraud alert or a free credit freeze with each bureau, change passwords, and review statements and reports. A freeze stops new accounts from being opened in your name until you lift it.

## Net worth, saving, and protection

Net worth equals assets minus liabilities. Track it once or twice a year. Compound growth rewards starting early: at 7%, money doubles about every 10 years by the rule of 72. An emergency fund of three to six months of essential expenses comes before most investing.

Protection includes insurance (health, auto, renters or home, disability, and life if others depend on your income) and basic estate documents. A will names who receives property and who cares for minor children. Beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and life insurance override a will for those accounts, so keep them current. A durable power of attorney and a health care directive name people to act if you cannot. Charitable giving can follow a budget line, and tax rules for deducting gifts change, so check the current year's rules.

> **Try it yourself**
>
> Jordan, 24, earns $3,200 a month after taxes. Expenses are rent $1,100, car payment $350, other essentials $900, and $400 of discretionary spending. Jordan has $1,500 in savings, a $4,000 card balance at 22% APR, a $9,000 car loan at 6%, and a car worth $11,000. (a) What is Jordan's net worth? (b) How much is left each month? (c) Recommend an order for using it.

## Key terms

**Adjusted gross income.** Total income minus certain adjustments; the starting point for deductions.

**Marginal tax rate.** The rate applied to the next dollar of taxable income.

**Deposit in transit.** A deposit recorded by the account holder that has not yet appeared on the bank statement.

**Credit freeze.** A free block on new credit accounts being opened using your credit report.

**Net worth.** Total assets minus total liabilities.
