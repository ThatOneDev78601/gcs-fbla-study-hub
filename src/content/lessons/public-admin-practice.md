## Public finance

Governments raise money from income taxes (mainly federal and state), sales taxes (mainly state and local), property taxes (the main local source, funding schools and services), fees and charges, intergovernmental grants, and borrowing. General obligation bonds are backed by the government's taxing power; revenue bonds are repaid from a specific project's income, such as tolls.

An operating budget pays for ongoing services such as salaries and supplies; a capital budget funds long-lived projects such as roads and buildings. Budget formats include line-item (by expense category), program (by service), performance-based (linking money to results), and zero-based (every expense justified from zero each cycle). The budget cycle is preparation by the executive, approval by the legislature or council, execution by departments, and audit. Most states and localities must balance their operating budgets.

Controls include appropriations (legal spending limits), encumbrances (reserving funds when orders are placed), procurement rules, and independent audits. Performance measures include inputs (money and staff), outputs (services delivered), outcomes (results for the public), and efficiency (cost per output).

> **Example**
>
> A city library's summer reading program costs $120,000 and serves 8,000 participants. 72% of participating children maintained or improved reading levels, compared with 55% of non-participants.
>
> Input: $120,000. Output: 8,000 participants. Efficiency: 120,000 / 8,000 = $15 per participant. Outcome: a 17-point difference in reading maintenance. The comparison suggests benefit, but families who choose to participate may differ, so a stronger evaluation would compare similar children.

## Policy design and evaluation

The policy cycle has five stages: agenda setting (a problem gains attention), formulation (options are developed), adoption (a legislature, council, or agency decides), implementation (agencies carry it out), and evaluation (results are measured and the policy is continued, changed, or ended). Evidence, budgets, public opinion, interest groups, courts, the media, and political leadership all influence decisions.

Agencies make rules under authority granted by statutes. The federal Administrative Procedure Act generally requires notice-and-comment rulemaking: publish the proposed rule, accept public comments, and publish the final rule with responses. States have similar laws. Courts can review whether an agency followed procedure and stayed within its authority.

## Public communication and access

Government communication must be clear to the whole public. The federal Plain Writing Act of 2010 requires agencies to use plain language in public documents. Write proposals with the problem, evidence, options, recommendation, cost, and how success will be measured, adapted to the audience: residents need practical steps, while council members need costs and tradeoffs. Digital services should be accessible to people with disabilities under Section 508 and related standards, available in needed languages, and usable on phones.

Public access laws make government transparent. The federal Freedom of Information Act and the Maryland Public Information Act give people the right to request government records, with specific exemptions. Maryland's Open Meetings Act requires most public bodies to meet in public with notice.

## Sectors, management, and accountability

Public managers plan, organize, staff, lead, and control like any manager, within civil service merit rules, union agreements, and public scrutiny. Innovation often starts as a pilot program with clear measures and a plan to scale or stop. Learning organizations use data and feedback to improve. The federal Hatch Act limits certain political activities by federal employees.

Ethics in public organizations centers on using public power for the public interest. Conflicts of interest arise when a decision could benefit an official, their family, or business; the usual responses are disclosure and recusal (stepping aside from the decision). Gift rules, financial disclosure, fair procurement, and whistleblower protections support accountability.

## Key terms

**General obligation bond.** Government debt backed by its full taxing power.

**Appropriation.** Legal authority to spend a specific amount for a purpose.

**Outcome measure.** A measure of the result a program produces for the public.

**Notice-and-comment rulemaking.** Publishing a proposed rule and considering public comments before finalizing it.

**Recusal.** Stepping aside from a decision because of a conflict of interest.
