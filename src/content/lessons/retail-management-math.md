## Price and markdown math

Retailers usually express markup as a percent of the retail (selling) price. Retail price = cost / (1 - markup percent). A sweater that costs $28 with a 60% initial markup sells for 28 / 0.40 = $70. A markdown is a price reduction. Markdown percent = markdown / original price. Reducing the $70 sweater to $52.50 is a $17.50 markdown, or 25%.

Maintained markup is the markup actually achieved after markdowns. If the sweater sells at $52.50, the maintained markup is (52.50 - 28) / 52.50 = 46.7%. Break-even units = fixed costs / (price - variable cost per unit). A store promotion costing $1,800 for an item with a $30 price and $18 cost must sell 1,800 / 12 = 150 extra units to break even.

| Measure | Formula | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retail price from cost | Cost / (1 - markup %) | $28 / 0.40 = $70 |
| Markdown % | Markdown / original price | $17.50 / $70 = 25% |
| Shrinkage % | Shrinkage / net sales | $7,500 / $500,000 = 1.5% |
| Stock turnover (at retail) | Net sales / average inventory at retail | $500,000 / $125,000 = 4 times |
| Stock-to-sales ratio | Beginning-of-month inventory / month's sales | $90,000 / $30,000 = 3.0 |

## Plan purchases with open-to-buy

Open-to-buy (OTB) is the amount a buyer can still order for a period without overstocking. Planned purchases equal planned sales plus planned end-of-month stock plus planned markdowns minus beginning-of-month stock. Subtract merchandise already on order to get open-to-buy. All figures are usually at retail value.

> **Example**
>
> For March, a department plans sales of $40,000, end-of-month stock of $60,000, and markdowns of $2,000. Beginning stock is $55,000, and $12,000 is already on order.
>
> 1. Planned purchases: 40,000 + 60,000 + 2,000 - 55,000 = $47,000.
> 2. Open-to-buy: 47,000 - 12,000 = $35,000.
>
> The buyer can order up to $35,000 more at retail. At a 55% planned markup, that is about 35,000 x 0.45 = $15,750 at cost.

## Assortment, brands, and allocation

Assortment width is the number of categories or brands carried; depth is the number of choices within each, such as sizes and colors. A specialty store is narrow and deep; a convenience store is broad and shallow. Many retailers offer good, better, and best quality levels and mix national brands with private labels, which earn higher margins.

Allocation divides incoming stock among stores based on each store's sales, space, and customer profile. If three stores sell 50%, 30%, and 20% of a category, a 300-unit shipment would be split 150, 90, and 60, then adjusted for current stock. Location tracking and unit control systems record each item by SKU, store, and bin so staff can find and replenish it. Shipping choices weigh speed, cost, and reliability, such as next-day freight for a fast-selling item versus slower, cheaper ground shipping for basics.

## Floor placement, planograms, and signage

A planogram is a diagram showing exactly where each product goes on a fixture, with shelf numbers, the number of facings (units visible across the front), and SKU positions. Reading one means matching each SKU to its shelf and facing count. Eye level is often called buy level; higher-margin or featured items go there, while bulky items go low. Cross-merchandising places related items together, such as chips beside salsa, to encourage add-on purchases.

Fixtures include gondolas, wall systems, round racks, four-way racks, tables, and endcaps. Signage ranges from storefront signs to department signs to shelf tags. Technology supports merchandising through sales data dashboards, electronic shelf labels, and customer databases that show which products and promotions each customer segment buys.

## Lead selling on the floor

Managers set daily sales goals, hold short staff huddles on promotions and new products, and watch the floor to coach associates in real time. Suggestion selling recommends a related item ("Would you like a protective case for that phone?"). Trading up offers a better version when it fits the customer's need. Demonstrations let the customer try the product. Handle objections by listening, acknowledging, and answering with facts. Retail documents such as price change lists, transfer forms, and damage reports keep records accurate.

> **Try it yourself**
>
> A jacket costs $45. (a) Find the retail price for a 55% initial markup. (b) After the season, it is marked down 30%. What is the new price, and what is the maintained markup percent? (c) The store's net sales for the year are $800,000 and shrinkage is $12,000. What is the shrinkage rate?

## Key terms

**Open-to-buy.** The amount of merchandise a buyer can still order for a period.

**Maintained markup.** The markup actually achieved after markdowns.

**Planogram.** A diagram showing where each product is placed on a fixture.

**Assortment depth.** The number of choices offered within a category.

**Cross-merchandising.** Displaying related products together to encourage add-on sales.
