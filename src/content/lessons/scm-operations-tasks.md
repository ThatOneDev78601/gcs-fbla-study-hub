## Purchasing and supplier relationships

Purchasing buys the materials and services the business needs at the right quality, quantity, time, and price. Methods include spot buying for one-time needs, blanket purchase orders that set terms for repeated purchases over a period, competitive bidding through requests for quotation (RFQ) or proposals (RFP), and e-procurement systems. Centralized purchasing gains volume discounts; decentralized purchasing responds faster to local needs.

Good purchasing raises productivity: reliable, on-time supplies keep production running, and quality materials reduce rework. Collaboration tools include shared forecasts and vendor-managed inventory, where the supplier monitors and refills the buyer's stock. Records include the purchase order, receiving report, invoice, and supplier performance data.

## Receive, store, pick, and ship

Warehouses can be private (owned by the company), public (rented space), or distribution centers built for fast movement. Cross-docking moves goods from incoming trucks straight to outgoing trucks with little or no storage. Order cycle time is the time from order placement to delivery; reducing any step, such as order processing, picking, or transit, shortens it.

1. Receiving: check quantities and condition against the purchase order and packing slip; record discrepancies.
2. Put-away: store items in assigned locations recorded in the warehouse system.
3. Picking: discrete picking fills one order at a time; batch picking collects items for several orders at once; zone picking assigns workers to areas; wave picking releases groups of orders at scheduled times.
4. Packing: choose the right box and packing material, include the packing slip, and label correctly.
5. Shipping: select the carrier, generate tracking, and confirm pickup.
6. Inventory update: the system records every movement so stock counts stay accurate.

## Inventory calculations

The economic order quantity (EOQ) balances ordering costs against holding costs: EOQ = square root of (2 x annual demand x cost per order / holding cost per unit per year). The reorder point tells when to order: daily demand x lead time in days, plus safety stock. ABC analysis classifies items by value: A items are the few that make up most of the inventory value and get the closest control.

> **Example**
>
> A distributor sells 12,000 units a year. Each order costs $50 to place, and holding one unit for a year costs $3. Daily demand is 40 units, lead time is 5 days, and safety stock is 60 units.
>
> 1. EOQ: square root of (2 x 12,000 x 50 / 3) = square root of 400,000 = about 632 units.
> 2. Orders per year: 12,000 / 632 = about 19.
> 3. Reorder point: 40 x 5 + 60 = 260 units.
>
> Order about 632 units whenever stock falls to 260. The safety stock covers demand spikes or delays during the 5-day lead time.

## Transportation and reverse logistics

Choose modes by product value, weight, urgency, distance, and cost. Assess carriers on on-time percentage, damage rate, cost per unit, and tracking. Global shipments add customs documents, Incoterms, and longer lead times.

Reverse logistics handles returns and end-of-life products: authorize the return, receive and inspect, then restock, refurbish, recycle, or dispose. Fast, clear returns improve customer loyalty and recover value. Distribution must follow laws such as hazardous materials shipping rules and driver hours-of-service limits, and ethical practice includes fair labor and honest delivery promises. Current trends include automation, nearshoring, real-time tracking, and lower-emission transport. Risks include single-source suppliers, port disruptions, and weather, managed with backup suppliers, safety stock, and contingency routes.

| Mode | Strengths | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truck (full or less-than-truckload) | Door-to-door, flexible | Higher cost per ton over long distances |
| Rail | Low cost for heavy, bulky goods over land | Fixed routes; needs trucks for the last leg |
| Water (ocean, barge) | Lowest cost for large volumes, global trade | Slowest; port delays |
| Air | Fastest for long distances | Most expensive; weight and size limits |
| Pipeline | Continuous flow of liquids and gas | Only certain products |
| Intermodal | Containers move between ship, rail, and truck | Coordination and transfer time |

> **Try it yourself**
>
> Annual demand is 7,200 units, ordering cost is $40, and holding cost is $4 per unit per year. Daily demand is 30 units, lead time is 6 days, and safety stock is 45. Calculate EOQ and the reorder point, then choose a transport mode for urgent replacement parts worth $2,000 each shipped across the country.

## Key terms

**Blanket purchase order.** An agreement setting terms for repeated purchases over a period.

**Cross-docking.** Transferring goods from inbound to outbound trucks with minimal storage.

**Economic order quantity.** The order size that minimizes total ordering and holding costs.

**Reorder point.** The inventory level that triggers a new order.

**Reverse logistics.** Managing the flow of returned or used products back through the supply chain.
