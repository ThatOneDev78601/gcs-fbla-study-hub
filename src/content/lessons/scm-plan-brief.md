## Analyze the brief first

Turn the brief into a requirement list: the product, customers and region, service commitments (delivery time, cost to the customer, returns), any donation or sustainability commitments, budget limits, and required report sections. Each later decision should point back to a requirement. This lesson uses a fictional company selling refillable cleaning kits, which is unrelated to the competition topic.

## Source ethically and choose suppliers

Supplier selection compares cost, quality, reliability, capacity, lead time, location, financial stability, and ethical practices such as fair labor and environmental standards. A weighted scorecard makes the comparison transparent. Request audits or certifications, ask about subcontractors, and avoid relying on a single supplier for a critical part.

Production planning sets what to make and when. Make-to-stock builds inventory ahead of demand; make-to-order builds after orders arrive. Packaging must protect the product, fit carrier size limits, meet labeling rules, and match sustainability commitments. Distribution choices range from one national warehouse (lower inventory, longer delivery) to several regional centers (faster delivery, more inventory and cost).

## Model costs and profit per order

Landed cost is the full cost of getting a unit to your warehouse: purchase price plus freight, duties, insurance, and handling. Fulfillment cost is the cost of getting an order to the customer: picking and packing labor, packaging, and shipping. Contribution per order is the price minus landed cost and fulfillment cost. Donation commitments, such as 5% of each sale, are another variable cost per order.

> **Example**
>
> A cleaning kit costs $6.00 from the supplier, plus $0.80 freight, $0.30 duty, and $0.10 insurance per unit. Fulfillment costs $1.50 labor, $0.90 packaging, and $4.20 shipping per order. The kit sells for $24, and the company donates 5% of the price.
>
> 1. Landed cost: 6.00 + 0.80 + 0.30 + 0.10 = $7.20.
> 2. Fulfillment cost: 1.50 + 0.90 + 4.20 = $6.60.
> 3. Donation: 24 x 5% = $1.20.
> 4. Contribution per order: 24.00 - 7.20 - 6.60 - 1.20 = $9.00.
>
> Each order contributes $9.00 toward fixed costs. Shipping is the largest single cost after the product, so distribution decisions strongly affect profit. Show this breakdown in the plan so judges see where each choice changes cost.

## Forecast demand and plan capacity

Forecasting methods include the moving average (average of recent periods), the weighted moving average (more weight on recent periods), and exponential smoothing (new forecast = old forecast + alpha x (actual - old forecast)). With monthly demand of 400, 460, and 520, a three-month average forecasts 460; weights of 0.5, 0.3, and 0.2 (newest first) forecast 0.5 x 520 + 0.3 x 460 + 0.2 x 400 = 478.

New products have no history, so use early signals: preorders, waitlist sign-ups, test-market sales, and similar products' launches. Limited releases create urgency but risk stockouts. Plan capacity responses before they are needed: safety stock, overtime, a second shift, a backup contract manufacturer, or pausing preorders. State what triggers each response, such as preorders exceeding 1,000 units.

## Link the plan to the rating criteria

Organize the final plan so each rating criterion has a clear section: the brief analysis, sourcing and supplier choice, production and packaging, distribution, the cost model, the demand forecast and capacity plan, risks, and how the plan meets service and sustainability commitments. Use consistent assumptions across every section, and present a base case and a stress case such as demand 30% above forecast or a supplier delay of two weeks.

## Key terms

**Landed cost.** The total cost of a product delivered to the buyer's warehouse, including freight and duties.

**Fulfillment cost.** The cost of picking, packing, and shipping an order to the customer.

**Weighted scorecard.** A tool that scores options on weighted criteria to compare them.

**Exponential smoothing.** A forecast that adjusts the previous forecast by a fraction of its error.

**Make-to-order.** Producing only after a customer order is received.
