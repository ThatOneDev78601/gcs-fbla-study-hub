## Set the goal and research the audience

Start with a business objective, then a campaign goal that serves it. "Increase weekday sales 15% in six weeks" is a business objective; "get 200 people to redeem a weekday offer" is a measurable campaign goal. Avoid goals that only count likes unless awareness is truly the objective.

Build one or two audience personas from real evidence: age range, location, interests, problems, where they spend time online, and what would make them act. Use platform audience data from reliable surveys, the business's own customer data, and short interviews. Choose platforms where the audience is active and the content format fits the product. One platform done well usually beats four done poorly.

This workshop uses a fictional neighborhood bakery launching a Saturday baking class. It is unrelated to the competition topics, so students build their own campaigns.

## Build the content plan and three connected ads

Content pillars are the three or four themes the account returns to, such as behind-the-scenes, tips, customer stories, and offers. A content calendar sets dates, formats, captions, and who produces each post. Mix original content with reused content, such as customer photos shared with permission and credit.

Connected ads share a visual style, a message, and a sequence. A common sequence moves the audience from awareness to consideration to action, with each ad doing one job. Captions open with a hook, give one key detail, and end with one call to action. Make content accessible: captions on every video, alt text for images, readable contrast, and hashtags written in camel case (#SaturdayBakeClass) so screen readers say them correctly.

| Ad | Job | Format | Hook and CTA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Awareness | 15-second video of dough being shaped, with captions | "Ever wanted to make bread that actually rises?" Learn more |
| 2 | Consideration | Carousel showing the class steps and the instructor | "In two hours, you'll bake a loaf and take it home." See class details |
| 3 | Action | Image of finished loaves with a limited-seat offer | "Only 12 seats each Saturday. First class 20% off with code BAKE20." Book now |

## Budget, schedule, and distribute

Distribution channels include organic posts, paid ads, partnerships with local creators (who must disclose the partnership), email, and in-store signs with a QR code. Search optimization matters on social platforms too: use the words people search, such as "baking class near me," in captions and profiles.

Allocate a limited budget to the ad that does the hardest job, usually the action ad aimed at people who already engaged. Schedule posts when the audience is active, and plan enough frequency to be remembered without repeating the same post too often. Build in a test: run two versions of one ad and shift budget to the better performer.

## Measure honestly

A forecast is a projection based on assumptions, such as industry-average click rates. Observed results are what actually happened. Label forecasts clearly, list the assumptions, and never present projected numbers as real results. After launch, compare observed results with the forecast and explain the adjustments you made.

| Metric | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Engagement rate | Engagements / reach (state which base you use) |
| Click-through rate (CTR) | Clicks / impressions |
| Cost per click (CPC) | Spend / clicks |
| CPM | Spend / impressions x 1,000 |
| Conversion rate | Conversions / clicks |
| Cost per acquisition (CPA) | Spend / conversions |
| Return on ad spend (ROAS) | Revenue from ads / ad spend |

> **Example**
>
> The bakery spends $500 on ads. They deliver 50,000 impressions and 1,000 clicks, leading to 40 class bookings at $30 each.
>
> 1. CPM: 500 / 50,000 x 1,000 = $10. CTR: 1,000 / 50,000 = 2%. CPC: 500 / 1,000 = $0.50.
> 2. Conversion rate: 40 / 1,000 = 4%. CPA: 500 / 40 = $12.50.
> 3. Revenue: 40 x 30 = $1,200. ROAS: 1,200 / 500 = 2.4.
>
> Each ad dollar returned $2.40 in revenue. Whether that is profitable depends on the class's costs; if ingredients and staff cost $15 per student, profit after ads is 1,200 - 600 - 500 = $100.

> **Try it yourself**
>
> Forecast a campaign: $300 budget, an assumed $8 CPM, an assumed 1.5% CTR, and an assumed 5% conversion rate. Estimate impressions, clicks, and conversions, then write one sentence that labels the forecast honestly.

## Key terms

**Content pillar.** A recurring theme that organizes a social media account's posts.

**Persona.** A research-based profile representing a target audience segment.

**Click-through rate.** Clicks divided by impressions.

**Return on ad spend.** Revenue generated by ads divided by the amount spent on them.

**Forecast.** A projected result based on stated assumptions, not an observed outcome.
