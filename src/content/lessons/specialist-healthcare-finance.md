## Follow the revenue cycle

The revenue cycle spans scheduling and registration through billing, payment and reconciliation. Accurate patient and coverage information helps prevent avoidable delays. Coding represents documented services in standardized forms; billing uses that information to submit a claim. A claim is a request for payment, not proof that payment is guaranteed. Payers review coverage, documentation and other requirements. Denials need analysis and an appropriate correction or appeal rather than repeated submission of unchanged errors. Distinguish the amount charged, the amount allowed by an agreement and the amount ultimately collected. These figures are not interchangeable.

## Insurance and budget basics

A premium maintains insurance coverage. A deductible is an amount a covered person may need to pay before specified benefits apply; a copayment is a stated charge and coinsurance is a percentage share. Actual obligations depend on the plan and service, so avoid universal promises. Administrators budget labor, supplies, facilities, technology and other costs. Fixed costs can remain over a relevant range of volume, while variable costs change with activity. Compare actual revenue and spending with the budget and investigate significant variances. Cash flow matters because a service may incur costs well before a claim is paid.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A simplified plan applies 20% coinsurance after the deductible is met to an allowed $500 service.
>
> The patient share under those assumptions is $100, and the payer share is $400. Do not apply the percentage to an unrelated list price or ignore remaining plan conditions.

## Policy, safeguards and public programs

HIPAA’s Privacy Rule governs protected health information use and disclosure; its Security Rule addresses electronic protected information through administrative, physical and technical safeguards. The Breach Notification Rule covers required notices after qualifying breaches. These rules apply to covered entities and business associates, not automatically to every person who knows a health fact. The Anti-Kickback Statute targets knowing, willful remuneration for federally reimbursed referrals or business; Stark addresses specified physician self-referrals involving financial relationships unless an exception applies. Medicare is federal insurance mainly for older adults and some eligible younger people. Medicaid is jointly funded federally and by states and administered by states under federal requirements. CMS administers major coverage programs within HHS; CDC focuses on public health. OSHA addresses worker safety, including bloodborne-pathogen exposure controls, training and safer work practices. Compliance translates these duties into policies, audits, reporting and corrective action.

## Technology, policy and trustworthy records

Electronic health records organize clinical information, while practice-management systems support scheduling and administrative workflows. Interoperability means systems can exchange and meaningfully use information, not merely send a file. Role-based access, audit logs, secure communication and appropriate retention protect sensitive records. Privacy laws and payer requirements must be checked through current authoritative guidance; a study summary cannot replace compliance advice. Common mistakes include assuming all staff need access to every record, treating software as a cure for poor documentation and using billing codes to imply services that records do not support. Accuracy and ethical stewardship connect finance, care and trust.

## Key terms to remember

**Claim.** A request to a payer for payment of a covered service.

**Coinsurance.** A percentage share of an eligible cost paid by the insured person.

**Interoperability.** The ability of systems to exchange and meaningfully use information.
