## Distribution connects the network

Distribution places products where and when customers need them. Warehouses store, handle and prepare goods; distribution centers often emphasize flow and order fulfillment. Receiving, put-away, picking, packing and shipping each create opportunities for error or delay. Layout and identification should support safe movement and accurate selection. Cross-docking transfers suitable goods from inbound to outbound movement with little storage, but it requires coordination. Packaging protects the product and communicates handling information, while its weight and volume affect cost. Distribution planning should consider customer location, order size, delivery frequency and product characteristics rather than simply minimizing the number of buildings.

## Compare transport options

Road, rail, water, air and pipeline offer different combinations of speed, flexibility, capacity and cost. Air is often fast but expensive; water can move large international volumes economically but slowly. Rail is useful for suitable high-volume routes, while road transport offers flexible local access. Intermodal transportation combines modes. Lead time includes more than travel: order processing, waiting, handling and customs can matter. Track reliability as well as average speed. A cheaper slow mode can require more inventory, so compare total logistics cost and service risk rather than freight price in isolation.

> **Example**
>
> A slower transport option saves $400 in freight but requires $550 more inventory and spoilage cost.
>
> Under those estimates, total relevant cost rises by $150. Choose using the full service and cost picture, not the freight saving alone.

## Track deliveries and returns

Useful measures include order accuracy, on-time delivery, damage, fulfillment time and total cost per order. Define 'on time' against an agreed promise rather than moving the deadline after delivery. Reverse logistics handles returns, repairs, reuse, recycling and disposal. Returned items require identification and condition checks before being put back into saleable stock. Plan for disruption through alternative routes, suitable buffers and clear communication. Common mistakes include speeding shipping to compensate for avoidable warehouse delays, ignoring last-mile constraints and assuming every return has no value. Trace the full order cycle to find the actual cause of missed service.

## Key terms

**Cross-docking.** Moving goods from inbound to outbound flow with little or no storage.

**Intermodal transport.** Movement using more than one transport mode.

**Reverse logistics.** Managing returns, repairs, reuse, recycling and disposal flows.
