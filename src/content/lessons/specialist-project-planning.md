## A project has an outcome and an ending

A project is a temporary effort that creates a unique result. Operations are ongoing activities that sustain the organization. A project charter authorizes the effort and summarizes purpose, objectives, major constraints and responsibility. Identify stakeholders early: they can influence the work or be affected by it. The sponsor supplies support and authority, while the project manager coordinates delivery. Define measurable success criteria and distinguish outputs from outcomes. Printing 200 brochures is an output; increasing attendance may be an outcome. Clear scope states what work is included so enthusiasm does not silently expand the commitment.

## Break work into manageable pieces

A work breakdown structure divides deliverables into smaller components. Estimate tasks, resources, dependencies and duration with the people who understand the work. A milestone marks an important point rather than a duration of effort. The critical path is the longest dependent path through the schedule; delay on it can delay the project unless the plan changes. Budget for realistic costs and contingency. Identify risks before they become issues, assign owners and plan responses. Predictive planning defines substantial work in advance; adaptive approaches use feedback and shorter cycles when requirements or solutions are uncertain.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A fundraiser needs two days of design, then three days of printing, then one day of distribution.
>
> Those dependent tasks require at least six days in sequence. Assigning more people to distribution does not shorten the earlier design and printing dependencies automatically.

## Common mistakes and test decisions

Write acceptance criteria that someone can verify: 'report contains a reconciled budget and three sourced recommendations' is stronger than 'report is great.' Build a communication plan around who needs which information and when. A responsibility matrix clarifies who performs, approves or advises on work, preventing duplicate effort and missing decisions. Do not confuse effort with elapsed duration or assume every task can run simultaneously. A risk is uncertain; an issue is already occurring. Planning should be sufficient for the project’s size and uncertainty, then updated as evidence changes rather than treated as an unchangeable prediction.

## Key terms to remember

**Scope.** The boundaries of the work and deliverables included in a project.

**Critical path.** The longest dependent sequence determining the minimum schedule duration.

**Stakeholder.** A person or group affecting or affected by a project.
