## Know whom the professional represents

Agency is a relationship in which an agent acts for a principal within authorized duties. A client is represented; a customer may participate without that same representation. The distinction affects what duties and services are owed. Fiduciary responsibilities commonly involve loyalty, care, accounting, disclosure, confidentiality and obedience to lawful instructions, but exact requirements vary. Authority can be limited, so an agent cannot promise actions outside the agreement. Clarify representation and obtain required disclosures before assumptions create a conflict. A commission arrangement by itself does not explain every duty or prove that every participant is represented.

## Disclosure, conflicts and fair dealing

Material information can influence a reasonable transaction decision. Know which facts require disclosure and which information must remain confidential under applicable rules. Do not invent an answer when uncertain; consult the responsible professional or current authoritative requirements. Representing parties with competing interests can create conflicts requiring special treatment or may be restricted. Fair housing principles prohibit unlawful discrimination in housing-related activity. Ethical communication focuses on the property and objective needs rather than steering people through stereotypes. Advertising should be truthful, authorized and sufficiently clear that a reasonable reader is not misled. Funds held for others require proper handling and records.

> **Example**
>
> A buyer asks a seller’s representative to reveal the seller’s lowest acceptable price.
>
> The representative should explain the representation relationship and protect confidential negotiating information unless disclosure is authorized or required. Helpfulness does not override duties to the principal.

## Fair housing and layered regulation

The federal Fair Housing Act identifies race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status and disability as protected bases. Discriminatory refusal, different terms and steering can affect access; state or local protections can add requirements. Federal lead-disclosure rules generally require known lead-paint and hazard information before sale or lease of most pre-1978 housing. Other disclosures depend on law and facts, so do not assume every hazard follows the same rule. Licensing, zoning, leases, tenant protections and local permits involve different regulatory authorities. Buyers’ agents represent buyers, sellers’ agents represent sellers, and dual or subagency arrangements require careful treatment under applicable rules.

## Checking duties and local requirements

Separate ethical principles from a claim about a specific state deadline, form or license rule. Use the current official event guide for the test’s expected scope and current regulators for real activity. When a question presents a conflict, identify the principal, duty, known fact and proper authorization before choosing a response. Common mistakes include assuming a friendly agent represents everyone, confusing confidentiality with permission to conceal a required material disclosure and following a client’s unlawful instruction. Accurate records and candid explanations protect all parties more effectively than vague assurances that everything will work out.

## Key terms

**Principal.** The person or organization represented in an agency relationship.

**Fiduciary duty.** A duty to act with the required loyalty and care for another’s interests.

**Material fact.** Information important enough to affect a reasonable decision.
