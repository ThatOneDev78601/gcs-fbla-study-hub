## A transaction has stages and conditions

A real-estate transaction commonly moves from negotiation to agreement, due diligence, financing and closing. A contract states the parties’ obligations, property, terms and conditions. A contingency makes performance depend on a specified event such as financing or inspection; exact rights depend on the agreement and governing law. Earnest money demonstrates commitment under agreed handling terms, while escrow involves a neutral arrangement for holding money or documents until conditions are met. Inspections evaluate condition; title work examines ownership-related claims. Closing reconciles documents and funds and completes the transfer according to applicable procedures.

## Understand mortgage and investment arithmetic

A mortgage loan creates a repayment obligation secured by property. Principal is the amount borrowed or remaining; interest is the borrowing charge. An amortizing payment gradually reduces principal, though the interest and principal shares change over time. Loan-to-value ratio divides loan amount by the property value used by the lender. Equity is property value minus outstanding secured debt, before selling costs and other adjustments. A down payment is not the same as total closing cash because fees and other items can also apply. For income property, net operating income subtracts operating expenses from operating revenue before financing and income taxes.

The secondary mortgage market involves trading existing mortgage loans or related securities, which can provide lenders with funds for further lending. It is distinct from the primary market where a borrower obtains a new loan.

> **Work through an example**
>
> A buyer finances $240,000 of a property valued at $300,000.
>
> Loan-to-value is $240,000 ÷ $300,000 = 80%. The $60,000 difference is a 20% down payment in this simplified example, before closing costs.

## Common mistakes and test decisions

Read the basis of every percentage: a commission calculated on sale price differs from an interest charge calculated on outstanding principal. Simple annual interest is principal multiplied by annual rate and time in years, but actual loan disclosures may use more complex payment conventions. Compare financing using total relevant costs and terms rather than a payment alone. Lower payments can result from a longer repayment period that increases total interest. Common mistakes include treating gross rent as profit, assuming approval removes every contingency and presenting an illustrative calculation as a current loan offer or individualized financial recommendation.

## Key terms to remember

**Principal.** The amount borrowed or remaining unpaid on a loan.

**Loan-to-value.** Loan amount divided by the property value used for lending.

**Contingency.** A contract condition tied to a specified event.
