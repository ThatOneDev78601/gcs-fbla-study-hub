## See the whole supply chain

A supply chain connects suppliers, producers, distributors, retailers and customers. Goods often move toward customers, while information and money move in several directions. Operations transform inputs into useful outputs. Supply-chain management coordinates these activities so local improvements do not damage the overall result. A factory can appear efficient by making enormous batches while creating excess inventory elsewhere. Forecasts estimate future demand; actual orders provide different evidence. The bullwhip effect describes amplified demand variation upstream when organizations react to distorted or delayed information. Shared timely information and sensible ordering policies can reduce avoidable swings.

## Inventory benefits and costs

Inventory can include raw materials, work in process, finished goods and supplies needed to operate. Cycle stock supports normal replenishment, while safety stock buffers uncertainty. Carrying inventory ties up money and creates storage, damage and obsolescence costs. Too little inventory can cause stockouts and lost service. A reorder point combines expected demand during replenishment lead time with any chosen safety stock. Inventory turnover compares cost of goods sold with average inventory value, using compatible accounting units. Physical counts and cycle counts check records against reality; accurate records are essential for dependable planning.

> **Example**
>
> A shop uses 12 units daily, receives replenishment after five days and keeps 15 units of safety stock.
>
> A simple reorder point is 12 × 5 + 15 = 75 units. This assumes the stated demand and lead time model; different uncertainty requires a more careful policy.

## Check the effect on the whole supply chain

Capacity is the output a process can sustain under stated conditions. A bottleneck constrains the flow, so improving a nonconstraint may not increase overall output. Lean approaches reduce waste and improve flow; just-in-time arrangements need reliable processes and suppliers rather than magical zero-risk inventory. Quality should be built into work, with checks that detect defects before they travel downstream. Common mistakes include treating all inventory as waste, comparing turnover using sales revenue against cost-valued inventory and maximizing machine utilization when customer demand does not need the extra output. Evaluate service, quality, cost and resilience together.

## Key terms

**Safety stock.** Inventory held as a buffer against uncertainty.

**Reorder point.** The inventory level that triggers a replenishment order.

**Bullwhip effect.** Amplification of demand variation as information and orders move upstream.
