## Industry structure and fans

Sports and entertainment includes professional, college, high school, youth, and recreational sports, plus concerts, theater, film, gaming and esports, and theme parks. Revenue comes from tickets, media rights, sponsorships, merchandise and licensing, concessions, parking, and premium seating. The core product, the game or show, is unpredictable, so marketers sell the whole experience: atmosphere, food, entertainment between plays, and social time.

Fans attend for entertainment, escape, social connection, family time, and identity with a team. Highly identified fans attend through losing seasons; casual fans respond to promotions and star players. Sponsorship gives a company association with the event in exchange for money or goods. Activation is what the sponsor does with the rights, such as a branded fan zone, and measures include audience reached, leads collected, and sales.

## Pricing, tickets, and distribution

Ticket pricing uses tiers by seat location, variable pricing by opponent or date set in advance, and dynamic pricing that changes with demand. Season tickets and group packages build a base of committed fans. Premium seating and suites earn high margins from businesses. A ticket campaign combines an offer (such as a family four-pack with hot dogs), a deadline, a channel, and a way to track sales.

Distribution covers how fans access the product: the venue, ticketing platforms and the resale market, broadcast and streaming, radio, and social media highlights. Advertising media include local TV and radio, digital and social ads, outdoor signs, and direct mail to past ticket buyers, which can be targeted by zip code and tracked with codes.

## Publicity and promotional production

A press release starts with "For Immediate Release" (or an embargo date), a headline, a dateline (city and date), and a lead paragraph answering who, what, when, where, and why. Body paragraphs add details and a quote from an official. It ends with a short boilerplate about the organization, contact information, and ### to mark the end. Newsletters keep season-ticket holders informed. Event programs list rosters, schedules, and sponsor ads.

A game-day script, or run of show, lists every moment with times: gates open, warm-ups, anthem, starting lineups, timeout promotions, halftime show, sponsor announcements, and post-game. Each line names the person responsible, the announcer's exact words, music, and video cues. Logos, tickets, and signage must follow brand standards and licensing rules. Celebrity appearances need a contract covering fee, schedule, travel, security, and approved activities.

> **Example**
>
> Write the first lines of a press release for a fictional minor-league team's new sensory-friendly game.
>
> "FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE. Riverton Otters Host First Sensory-Friendly Game. RIVERTON, Md. (April 2, 2027) The Riverton Otters will host the league's first sensory-friendly game on May 15 at 1 p.m. at Harbor Field, with lowered music volume, no fireworks, and a quiet room staffed by trained volunteers, so that fans with sensory sensitivities can enjoy baseball with their families." A quote, details on tickets, the boilerplate, and a media contact follow.

## Agents, governing bodies, rights, contracts, and risk

Agents negotiate contracts and endorsements for athletes and entertainers, owe them a fiduciary duty, and in major leagues must be certified by the players' association. Governing bodies set rules and eligibility: leagues, the NCAA and other college associations, state high school associations, and international federations. College athletes have been able to earn money from their name, image, and likeness (NIL) since 2021, under evolving rules.

Rights that can be sold include naming rights, broadcast rights, and licensing rights for merchandise. Contracts must state the parties, obligations, payment, term, and what happens on breach. Barter trades goods or services instead of cash, such as tickets for radio advertising. Insurance includes general liability, event cancellation, and coverage for property and participants. A risk assessment lists hazards (weather, crowd crush, injury), their likelihood and impact, and controls. Event readiness checks cover staffing, security, medical services, signage, and backup plans.

## Economic impact

Economic impact estimates how much new money an event brings into a community. Count only spending by visitors from outside the area, since local residents would likely have spent that money locally anyway. Direct spending times a multiplier estimates total impact as the money circulates through local businesses. Multipliers vary by study, so state the assumption.

> **Try it yourself**
>
> A weekend youth soccer tournament draws 8,000 visitors from outside the county who spend an average of $120 each on lodging, food, and shopping, and 3,000 local spectators who spend $40 each. Using a multiplier of 1.6, estimate the economic impact and explain which spending you excluded.

## Key terms

**Sponsorship activation.** The activities a sponsor uses to promote its association with an event.

**Dynamic pricing.** Ticket prices that change with demand in real time.

**Run of show.** A timed script of every element of an event.

**Naming rights.** The right to put a company's name on a venue or event.

**Economic multiplier.** A factor estimating how new spending circulates through a local economy.
