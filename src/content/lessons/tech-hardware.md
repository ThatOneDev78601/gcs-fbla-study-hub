## Follow information through the computer

Input devices capture information; processors execute instructions; memory holds the active working set; storage preserves files. RAM is volatile, so turning off power loses its contents. An SSD keeps data without power and normally improves file loading, but it cannot replace adequate RAM for many simultaneous applications. A CPU performs general processing, while a GPU handles highly parallel work such as graphics. The motherboard connects components through buses and expansion interfaces. A power supply converts incoming power into the voltages components require. Choose compatible sockets, memory generations and power requirements before upgrading.

## Connections, mobile devices and printing

A connector’s shape does not guarantee its capabilities: a USB-C port may support different data speeds, charging levels or video features. Check specifications and test a known-good cable. Bluetooth handles nearby wireless peripherals; Wi-Fi connects devices to a local wireless network. Mobile systems balance performance against battery life and thermal limits. A swollen battery requires trained handling, not squeezing or puncturing. Printers can fail at the application, queue, driver, connection or mechanical stage. Laser printers use toner; inkjet printers spray liquid ink. A document appearing in a queue proves submission, not successful printing.

> **Example**
>
> A laptop works normally but its external monitor shows no image through a new USB-C cable.
>
> Check whether the port and cable support video, select the correct monitor input, and test a known-good supported connection before replacing the display.

## Diagnosing hardware faults

Start with safe, reversible checks: confirm power, inspect connections, read the exact error and reproduce the symptom. Shut down and follow manufacturer procedures before internal service; protect components from electrostatic discharge. Never open a power supply as a routine troubleshooting step. Distinguish capacity from speed: a nearly full disk and insufficient RAM can both slow a computer for different reasons. For printing, test another document and inspect the queue before reinstalling everything. A peripheral working on another computer narrows the cause toward the original device, driver or connection rather than proving every component healthy.

## Key terms

**RAM.** Volatile working memory used by active programs.

**Driver.** Software that lets an operating system communicate with hardware.

**Peripheral.** An attached input, output or storage device.
