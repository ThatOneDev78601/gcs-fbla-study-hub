## Threats, vulnerabilities, and risk

An asset is something valuable, such as customer records or service availability. A threat is a possible cause of harm; a vulnerability is a weakness a threat could exploit. Risk combines the likelihood and impact of that harm. Confidentiality limits disclosure, integrity protects accuracy and authorized change, and availability keeps services usable. A stolen password can affect all three. Controls reduce risk but rarely remove it completely. A risk assessment considers the asset, plausible attacks, existing controls and consequences before choosing a response. Technical sophistication alone does not determine business impact.

## Common attacks and warning signs

Phishing uses deceptive messages to prompt actions such as revealing credentials or opening malicious content. Social engineering exploits human trust, urgency or authority. Malware includes ransomware that denies access to data, spyware that collects information and other malicious programs. Unpatched software, weak access controls and unsafe defaults create opportunities. SQL injection occurs when untrusted input changes the meaning of a database query; parameterized queries separate data from executable instructions. Cross-site scripting can make untrusted content run as browser code. Defensive testing requires explicit authorization and a defined scope; curiosity is not permission to attack a real system.

> **Example**
>
> An urgent email claims the principal needs gift cards and asks you to bypass normal approval.
>
> Verify through a known independent contact channel and follow purchasing procedures. The claimed authority and urgency are warning signs, not reasons to skip verification.

## Assessing the risk

Distinguish an accidental disclosure from a deliberate intrusion, while recognizing that either can cause harm. Assess suspicious links without entering credentials or opening attachments. Report suspected incidents using the organization’s process. Security awareness is a control, but blaming users does not replace safer systems. Treating every risk as equally important wastes resources; prioritize severe plausible consequences. Risk can be reduced, avoided, transferred or accepted by an authorized decision maker. Insurance may transfer some financial consequences but does not prevent an attack or repair trust. Reassess as systems, threats and business needs change.

## Key terms

**Vulnerability.** A weakness that can be exploited or triggered to cause harm.

**Integrity.** Protection against unauthorized or accidental improper alteration.

**Social engineering.** Manipulation of people to obtain access or induce unsafe actions.
