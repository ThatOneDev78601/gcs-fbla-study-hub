## Count the correct denominator

A majority is more than half; a two-thirds requirement is at least two thirds, without rounding down. A plurality is the largest number and need not be a majority. Under ordinary votes-cast rules, abstentions are not votes; rules based on members present or total membership behave differently. Check the stated denominator before calculating. Tie votes fail when a majority is required.

> **Work through an example**
>
> Seventeen votes are cast. How many affirmative votes are needed?
>
> A majority needs nine. Two thirds of 17 is 11.33, so a two-thirds requirement needs twelve. Do not round down.

## Choose a method and conduct elections

Voice votes suit ordinary questions; rising or counted votes clarify results; ballots can protect secrecy and may be required by rules. A division requests a rising vote under the applicable procedure; it is not automatically a demand for a roll call. Nominations propose candidates, while election votes select them. A presiding member’s voting rights depend on the body, method, and rules; “the chair only votes to break ties” is an inaccurate universal rule. Do not assume plurality elects when a majority is required.

Unanimous consent allows routine action when the chair asks for objection and no one objects. An objection does not itself defeat the proposal; it requires handling it through the regular procedure. In a large assembly, the chair ordinarily remains impartial and votes by ballot or when the vote affects the result under the applicable rules. Small boards and committees can follow different procedures.

## Use committees and boards within authority

Standing committees handle continuing work; special committees serve a defined task. Referral sends a pending matter for study and recommendation as authorized. A committee report can recommend action, but the assembly may still need to adopt the recommendation. Boards operate under powers granted by the governing documents; they are not automatically above the membership in every matter. Record scope, deadline, resources, and reporting responsibility so delegation does not become unexplained authority.

## Key terms to remember

**Majority.** More than half of the applicable voting base.

**Standing committee.** A committee for continuing responsibilities.
